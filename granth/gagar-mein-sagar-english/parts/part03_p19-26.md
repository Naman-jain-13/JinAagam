**Question 8.** What are the 85 sub-types of tiryancha (animal/plant beings)?
Answer: The sammurcchana (spontaneously-born, non-sexually generated) tiryanchas have 69 sub-types and the garbhaja (womb-born) tiryanchas have 16; together these make 85 sub-types of tiryancha.

**Question 9.** What are the 69 jiva-samasa (soul-classes) of sammurcchana tiryancha?
Answer: Ekendriya (one-sensed beings) have 42, vikalatraya (the two-, three- and four-sensed beings) have 9, and sammurcchana panchendriya (five-sensed) tiryanchas have 18 — together making 69.

**Question 10.** What are the 42 sub-types of ekendriya?
Answer: Earth-bodied, water-bodied, fire-bodied, air-bodied, nitya-nigoda and itara-nigoda beings — these six, considered as bādara (gross) and sūkṣma (subtle), give 12 types. Adding sapratiṣṭhita (with a co-resident basis) and apratiṣṭhita (without one) vegetable-bodied beings makes 14. Multiplying these 14 by paryāptaka (fully developed), nivṛtti-paryāptaka (formation-complete) and labdhi-aparyāptaka (potentially undeveloped) gives 42 sub-types.

**Question 11.** What are the 9 sub-types of vikalatraya?
Answer: Two-sensed, three-sensed and four-sensed beings, each multiplied by paryāptaka, nivṛtti-paryāptaka and labdhi-aparyāptaka, give the 9 sub-types of vikalatraya.

**Question 12.** What are the 18 sub-types of sammurcchana panchendriya?
Answer: Water-dwellers, land-dwellers and sky-dwellers, each considered as saṁjñī (rational/possessing a mind) and asaṁjñī (non-rational), give 6 types. Multiplying these 6 by paryāptaka, nivṛtti-paryāptaka and labdhi-aparyāptaka gives 18 sub-types.

**Question 13.** What are the 16 sub-types of garbhaja (womb-born) panchendriya?
Answer: Karmabhūmi (land-of-action) beings have 12 and bhogabhūmi (land-of-enjoyment) beings have 4; together these make the 16 sub-types of garbhaja panchendriya.

**Question 14.** What are the 12 sub-types of karmabhūmi (beings)?
Answer: Water-dwellers, land-dwellers and sky-dwellers, considered as saṁjñī and asaṁjñī, give 6 types. Considering these as paryāptaka and nivṛtti-paryāptaka gives 12 sub-types.

**Question 15.** What are the four sub-types of bhogabhūmi (beings)?
Answer: Land-dwellers and sky-dwellers, considered as paryāptaka and nivṛtti-paryāptaka, give four sub-types. In bhogabhūmi there are no water-dwelling or asaṁjñī tiryanchas.

**Question 16.** What are the nine sub-types of humans?
Answer: Ārya-khaṇḍa, Mleccha-khaṇḍa, bhogabhūmi and kubhogabhūmi (inferior land-of-enjoyment) humans — these four types of garbhaja (womb-born) humans, considered as paryāptaka and nivṛtti-paryāptaka, give 8 types. Adding the sammurcchana (spontaneously-born) human labdhi-aparyāptaka type makes nine.

**Question 17.** What are the two sub-types of hell-beings (naraki)?
Answer: Paryāptaka and nivṛtti-paryāptaka.

**Question 18.** What are the two sub-types of devas?
Answer: Paryāptaka and nivṛtti-paryāptaka.

**Question 19.** How are the 19 jiva-samasas (classes of souls) arrived at?
Answer: Earth-bodied, water-bodied, fire-bodied, air-bodied, itara-nigoda and nitya-nigoda beings — these six, considered as sūkṣma (subtle) and bādara (gross), give 12 types. Adding two types of vegetable-bodied beings — sapratiṣṭhita pratyeka and apratiṣṭhita pratyeka — gives 14 types of sthāvara (immobile beings) in all. Adding the three vikalatraya classes and the saṁjñī and asaṁjñī panchendriya classes brings the total to 19.

**Question 20.** How are the 57 jiva-samasas arrived at?
Answer: Considering each of the above 19 types as paryāpta, aparyāpta and labdhi-aparyāpta gives 57 sub-types.

# 14. Margana (The Fourteen Categories of Soul-Quest)

**Question 1.** What is margana (soul-quest)? How many are there?
Answer: The investigation (search) of souls in their various modes of existence, by means of their psychic dispositions (bhava), is called margana. There are fourteen of these.

**Question 2.** Name the marganas.
Answer: Gati (destiny), indriya (sense), kaya (body-class), yoga (activity), veda (sex-disposition), kashaya (passion), jnana (knowledge), samyam (self-restraint), darshan (perception), leshya (thought-colouration), bhavyatva (fitness for liberation), samyaktva (right faith), samjni (rationality) and aharaka (nutrition-intake).

**Question 3.** What is gati? How many are there? Name them.
Answer: The particular state of a soul attained through the rise of jāti-nāmakarma (destiny-determining karma) is called gati. There are four: naraka-gati (hell), tiryancha-gati (animal/plant), manushya-gati (human), deva-gati (celestial).

**Question 4.** What is indriya (sense organ)? How many are there? Name them.
Answer: The particular external mark of a worldly soul, arising through the kṣayopaśama (destruction-cum-subsidence) of indriyāvaraṇa karma (sense-obscuring karma), is called an indriya. There are five: touch (sparshana), taste (rasana), smell (ghrana), sight (chakshu), and hearing (karna).

**Question 5.** How many kinds of indriya are there? Name them.
Answer: There are two kinds of indriya — (1) dravya-indriya (physical sense organ) and (2) bhava-indriya (psychic sense organ).

**Question 6.** What is dravya-indriya?
Answer: Nivritti (the physical formation) and upakarana (the auxiliary structure) together are called dravya-indriya.

**Question 7.** What is bhava-indriya?
Answer: Labdhi (the capacity/attainment) and upayoga (the cognitive application) together are called bhava-indriya.

**Question 8.** What is nivritti? How many kinds are there?
Answer: The particular configuration of the soul's space-points [that forms a sense organ] is called nivritti. It is of two kinds: bāhya-nivritti (external formation) and abhyantara-nivritti (internal formation).

**Question 9.** What is bāhya-nivritti (external formation)?
Answer: The particular configuration of pudgala (matter) in the shape of a sense organ is called bāhya-nivritti.

**Question 10.** What is abhyantara-nivritti (internal formation)?
Answer: The particular configuration, in the shape of a sense organ, of the soul's own purified space-points is called abhyantara-nivritti.

**Question 11.** What is upakarana (the auxiliary organ)? How many kinds are there? Name them.
Answer: That which protects/assists the nivritti is upakarana. It is of two kinds — (1) bāhya (external) (2) abhyantara (internal).

**Question 12.** What is bāhya (external) upakarana?
Answer: That which assists the nivritti the way the eyelid assists the organ of sight.

**Question 13.** What is abhyantara (internal) upakarana?
Answer: That which assists the nivritti in every sense organ the way the black and white portions [of the pupil] assist the organ of sight.

**Question 14.** What is labdhi?
Answer: The kṣayopaśama (destruction-cum-subsidence) of jñānāvaraṇa karma (knowledge-obscuring karma) is called labdhi.

**Question 15.** What is upayoga?
Answer: The particular modification of consciousness that results from that kṣayopaśama is called upayoga.

**Question 16.** What is kaya (body-class)?
Answer: The mode of existence arising through the rise of trasa-nāmakarma and sthāvara-nāmakarma (mobile-being and immobile-being determining karma) is called kaya. The mass of matter accumulated through yoga (activity) is called kaya-margana.

**Question 17.** How many kinds of kaya are there?
Answer: There are six kinds of kaya: earth, water, fire, air, vegetation, and trasa (mobile beings) — that is, the five sthāvara (immobile) classes and one trasa class.

**Question 18.** What is yoga? How many kinds are there?
Answer: The power, arising through the rise of body-determining karma, that causes a soul possessed of mind, speech and body to draw in karmas, is called yoga. There are three: mind (mana), speech (vachana), body (kaya).

**Question 19.** How many sub-types does yoga-margana have? Name them.
Answer: Yoga-margana has 15 sub-types. Mano-yoga (mental activity) and vachana-yoga (vocal activity) each have 4 (true, untrue, mixed, neither-true-nor-untrue), and kaya-yoga (bodily activity) has 7 — audarika (physical), audarika-mishra (physical-mixed), vaikriyika (transformable), vaikriyika-mishra (transformable-mixed), aharaka (projectable), aharaka-mishra (projectable-mixed) and karmana (karmic).

**Question 20.** What is veda (sex-disposition)?
Answer: The desire for sexual union that arises in a soul through the rise of the no-kashaya called veda is called bhava-veda (psychic gender). The particular bodily mark manifested through nama-karma is called dravya-veda (physical gender).

**Question 21.** How many kinds of veda are there?
Answer: Stri-veda (female), purusha-veda (male), napumsaka-veda (neuter).

**Question 22.** What is kashaya (passion)?
Answer: That which harms the soul's states of samyaktva (right faith), partial conduct, complete conduct and yathakhyata (ideal) conduct is called kashaya.

**Question 23.** How many kinds of kashaya are there?
Answer: There are twenty-five: anantanubandhi (endless-binding — anger, pride, deceit, greed), apratyakhyana (partial-vow-obstructing — anger, pride, deceit, greed), pratyakhyana (full-vow-obstructing — anger, pride, deceit, greed), sanjvalana (subtle-conduct-obstructing — anger, pride, deceit, greed), plus laughter, liking, disliking, sorrow, fear, disgust, female-gender, male-gender and neuter-gender. [This makes] 16 kashayas and 9 no-kashayas.

**Question 24.** What is jnana (knowledge)?
Answer: That quality of the soul by which it knows objects pertaining to the three times [past, present, future] is called jnana (the knowledge-quality).

**Question 25.** How many kinds of jnana-margana are there?
Answer: Jnana-margana has eight kinds: mati-jnana (sensory/rational knowledge), shruta-jnana (scriptural knowledge), avadhi-jnana (clairvoyance), manahparyaya-jnana (mind-reading knowledge), kevala-jnana (omniscience), ku-mati-jnana, ku-shruta-jnana and ku-avadhi-jnana (their respective false/perverted counterparts).

**Question 26.** How many are the valid means of knowledge (pramana-jnana)?
Answer: Mati, shruta, avadhi, manahparyaya and kevala-jnana — these constitute right knowledge (samyag-jnana), which is pramana (a valid means of knowledge).

**Question 27.** How many limbs (anga) does samyag-jnana (right knowledge) have? What are they?
Answer: Samyag-jnana has eight limbs: 1. vyanjana-vyanjitaya, 2. artha-samagraya, 3. tadubhaya-samagraya, 4. kaladhyayanaya, 5. upadhyanopahitaya, 6. vinaya-labdhi-prabhavanaya, 7. guru-vadahvaya, 8. bahumanonmanaya.

**Question 28.** What is vyanjana-vyanjitaya?
Answer: Reading the letters and consonants correctly.

**Question 29.** What is artha-samagraya?
Answer: Pronouncing correctly along with the meaning.

**Question 30.** What is tadubhaya-samagraya?
Answer: Reading the letters and consonants together with the meaning.

**Question 31.** What is kaladhyayanaya?
Answer: Studying scripture only during its prescribed time, and not studying it at an improper time.

**Question 32.** What is upadhyanopahitaya?
Answer: Not abandoning a scriptural text one has begun studying until it is completed.

**Question 33.** What is vinaya-labdhi-prabhavanaya?
Answer: Showing reverence to the Jina's teaching (Jinavani), and studying with due reverence.

**Question 34.** What is guru-vadahvaya?
Answer: Not concealing the name of the teacher from whom one learned.

**Question 35.** What is bahumanonmanaya?
Answer: Showing great reverence toward one's teacher.

**Question 36.** What is samyam (self-restraint)?
Answer: Taking vows, observing the samitis (rules of careful conduct), restraining the passions, renouncing sensual enjoyments, and conquering the senses — this is called samyam.

**Question 37.** How many kinds of samyam-margana are there? Name them.
Answer: Samyam-margana has 7 kinds: samayika, chhedopasthapana, parihara-vishuddhi, sukshma-samparaya, yathakhyata, samyamasamyam, and asamyam.

**Question 38.** What is darshan (perception)?
Answer: Grasping only the general aspect — not the particular aspect — of an object that has both general and particular characteristics is called darshan. It is of 4 kinds — (1) chakshu-darshan (visual perception) (2) achakshu-darshan (non-visual perception) (3) avadhi-darshan (clairvoyant perception) (4) kevala-darshan (perfect perception).

**Question 39.** What is leshya (thought-colouration)? How many kinds are there?
Answer: The activity of yoga as coloured by kashaya (passion) is called leshya. It has six kinds: 1. krishna (black) 2. nila (blue) 3. kapota (grey) 4. peeta (yellow) 5. padma (pink) 6. shukla (white).

**Question 40.** What is bhavya-margana? How many kinds are there?
Answer: It is the investigation of whether a worldly soul has the inherent fitness (bhavyatva) to attain liberation or not. It has two kinds: 1. bhavyatva (fit for liberation) 2. abhavyatva (never fit for liberation).

**Question 41.** What is samyaktva-margana? How many kinds does it have?
Answer: Firm faith in the seven tattvas (fundamental realities) as taught by the Jina is called samyaktva. It has six kinds: 1. upashama (subsidential) 2. kshayopashamika (destructive-cum-subsidential) 3. kshayika (purely destructive) 4. mithyatva (wrong faith) 5. sasadana (faith on the verge of falling) 6. mishra (mixed).

**Question 42.** What is upashama-samyaktva?
Answer: The samyaktva that arises from the subsidence of the four anantanubandhi kashayas together with mithyatva, samyagmithyatva and samyak-prakriti — these seven karmic natures.

**Question 43.** What is kshayopashamika (vedaka) samyaktva?
Answer: The samyaktva that arises from the operative-destruction (udayabhavi-kshaya) of the four anantanubandhi kashayas together with mithyatva and samyagmithyatva — these six natures — combined with their subsidence-in-potential-existence and the rise of the samyaktva-prakriti.

**Question 44.** What is kshayika samyaktva?
Answer: The samyaktva that arises from the complete destruction of the four anantanubandhi kashayas together with the three natures of darshana-mohaniya (faith-deluding karma) — these seven natures.

**Question 45.** What is samjni (rational)? How many kinds are there?
Answer: The knowledge produced by the kshayopashama of no-indriyavarana karma (mind-obscuring karma) is called samjna (mind). One who possesses this samjna (mind) is called samjni, and one who lacks mind is called asamjni.

**Question 46.** What is ahara-margana (nutrition-quest)? How many kinds are there?
Answer: The taking-in of the matter-particles (ahara-vargana) suitable for forming the body and completing the paryaptis (developmental capacities) is called ahara-margana. It has two kinds: 1. aharaka (nutrition-taking) 2. anaharaka (non-nutrition-taking).

**Question 47.** How many are the sub-types (uttara-bheda) of the fourteen marganas?
Answer: There are 95 sub-types.

# 15. Dravya (Substance)

**Question 1.** What is dravya (substance)?
Answer: The aggregate of qualities (gunas) is called dravya. Origination, cessation and permanence (utpada, vyaya, dhrauvya) together constitute dravya. That which possesses qualities (guna) and modes (paryaya) is called dravya.

**Question 2.** How many dravyas are there?
Answer: There are six dravyas: 1. jiva (soul), 2. pudgala (matter), 3. dharma (medium of motion), 4. adharma (medium of rest), 5. akasha (space) and 6. kala (time).

**Question 3.** How many dravyas are murtika (corporeal)?
Answer: Only one dravya, pudgala, is corporeal.

**Question 4.** How many dravyas have asankhyata (innumerable) space-points?
Answer: Four dravyas — jiva, pudgala, dharma, adharma.

**Question 5.** How many dravyas are parinami (subject to modification) and sakriya (active/in motion)? Name them.
Answer: Two dravyas — jiva and pudgala.

**Question 6.** Which dravyas are, in a certain sense, single (only one of their kind)?
Answer: Dharma, adharma, akasha.

**Question 7.** In what respect is jiva dravya said to be corporeal?
Answer: In respect of its association with karmas, jiva dravya is called corporeal.

**Question 8.** Define jiva dravya.
Answer: That in which the characteristic of knowledge-and-perception-consciousness is found.

**Question 9.** What is pudgala dravya?
Answer: That in which touch, taste, smell and colour are found.

**Question 10.** How many kinds of pudgala dravya are there? Name them.
Answer: Paramanu (atom) and skandha (aggregate/molecule).

**Question 11.** What is a paramanu (atom)?
Answer: The smallest part of pudgala — one that cannot be further divided or broken — is called a paramanu. It occupies a single space-point.

**Question 12.** What is a skandha (aggregate)?
Answer: A collection of two or more paramanus is called a skandha. Skandhas may occupy a numerable, innumerable or even infinite number of space-points.

**Question 13.** What is dharma dravya?
Answer: That which, while itself motionless, assists jiva and pudgala in moving.

**Question 14.** What is adharma dravya?
Answer: That which, while itself motionless, assists jiva and pudgala in coming to rest.

**Question 15.** What is akasha (space) dravya?
Answer: That which provides room for all substances to exist in.

**Question 16.** How many kinds of akasha are there?
Answer: There are two kinds: loka-akasha (the space occupied by the universe) and aloka-akasha (space beyond it).

**Question 17.** What is loka-akasha?
Answer: That space in which all six dravyas exist is called loka-akasha. It has three parts: adho-loka (lower world), madhya-loka (middle world), urdhva-loka (upper world).

**Question 18.** What is aloka-akasha?
Answer: The space lying beyond loka-akasha is called aloka-akasha.

**Question 19.** What is kala (time) dravya?
Answer: That which assists all substances in their modification (parinamana) is called kala. It has two aspects — nishchaya-kala (real/absolute time) and vyavahara-kala (conventional time). The kalanu (atom of time) is called nishchaya-kala, while units such as the ghadi, ghanta (hour), day and night are called vyavahara-kala.

**Question 20.** What is the distinguishing quality of each of the six dravyas?
Answer: Jiva dravya — the characteristic of consciousness. Pudgala dravya — touch, taste, smell, colour. Dharma dravya — the property of being an aid to motion. Adharma dravya — the property of being an aid to rest. Akasha dravya — the property of providing space (avagahana). Kala dravya — the property of being an aid to modification (parinamana).

**Question 21.** What are the general (samanya) qualities common to all dravyas? Name them.
Answer: Existence (astitva), thing-hood (vastutva), substance-hood (dravyatva), knowability (prameyatva), non-heaviness/non-lightness (agurulaghutva), space-point-hood (pradeshatva), non-consciousness (achetanatva) and formlessness (amurtatva), etc.

# 16. Tattva (The Fundamental Realities)

**Question 1.** What is tattva? How many kinds are there? Name them.
Answer: The true nature of a thing is called tattva. There are seven: jiva (soul), ajiva (non-soul), asrava (influx of karma), bandha (bondage), samvara (stoppage of influx), nirjara (partial shedding of karma) and moksha (liberation).

**Question 2.** What is jiva-tattva?
Answer: That in which knowledge and perception (consciousness) are found is called jiva.

**Question 3.** What is ajiva-tattva?
Answer: That in which knowledge and perception (consciousness) are not found is called ajiva.

**Question 4.** What is asrava-tattva?
Answer: The inflow of karmas [into the soul] is called asrava. It has two kinds — dravya-asrava, bhava-asrava.

**Question 5.** What is dravya-asrava?
Answer: The transformation of karmic matter-particles (karmana varganas) into the form of karma, as they come in, is called dravya-asrava.

**Question 6.** What is bhava-asrava?
Answer: The soul's disposition of attachment and aversion (raga-dvesha) is called bhava-asrava.

**Question 7.** What is bandha-tattva (bondage)? How many kinds does it have? Name them.
Answer: The soul's space-points and the karmic space-points occupying the same location is called bandha-tattva. It has two kinds — dravya-bandha, bhava-bandha.

**Question 8.** Define dravya-bandha and bhava-bandha.
Answer: The occupying of the same space by karmana varganas and the soul is dravya-bandha, while the psychic states owing to which this bondage occurs are called bhava-bandha.

**Question 9.** What is samvara-tattva?
Answer: The stopping of asrava is samvara. It has two kinds — dravya-samvara, bhava-samvara.

**Question 10.** What are dravya-samvara and bhava-samvara?
Answer: The non-arrival of karmana varganas is dravya-samvara, while the psychic states owing to which the disposition of attachment-aversion is halted and the inflow of karmana varganas stops are called bhava-samvara.

**Question 11.** What is nirjara-tattva?
Answer: The partial shedding of karmas from the soul is called nirjara. It has two kinds — dravya-nirjara, bhava-nirjara.

**Question 12.** What is moksha-tattva?
Answer: The complete separation of dravya-karma, bhava-karma and no-karma (quasi-karmic matter) from the soul is called moksha. It has two kinds — dravya-moksha and bhava-moksha.

**Question 13.** Define dravya-moksha and bhava-moksha.
Answer: The complete destruction of dravya-karmas [from the soul] is dravya-moksha, and the psychic states by which all the soul's karmas are destroyed is called bhava-moksha.

**Question 14.** Which are the upadeya (worth-adopting) tattvas?
Answer: Jiva, samvara, nirjara and moksha.

**Question 15.** Which are the heya (worth-rejecting) tattvas?
Answer: Ajiva, asrava and bandha.

**Question 16.** List the categories of padartha (the fundamental entities) by name.
Answer: Jiva, ajiva, asrava, bandha, samvara, nirjara, moksha, punya (merit) and papa (demerit/sin).

**Question 17.** What is punya (merit)?
Answer: That which purifies the soul is called punya.

**Question 18.** What is papa (sin)?
Answer: That which defiles (degrades) the soul is called papa.

**Question 19.** Within which tattva are punya and papa included?
Answer: Within the asrava tattva.

**Question 20.** What are the causes of asrava?
Answer: Mithyatva (wrong faith), avirati (lack of restraint), pramada (carelessness), kashaya (passion), and yoga (activity).

**Question 21.** How many kinds of mithyatva are there?
Answer: Five kinds — viparita (perverse), ekanta (one-sided), vinaya (excessive-deference), samshaya (doubting), and ajnana (ignorance-based).

**Question 22.** What is avirati?
Answer: Not showing compassion toward the beings of the six kayas (the six classes of living bodies), and not bringing the five senses and the mind under control — this is avirati.

**Question 23.** What is pramada? How many kinds does it have?
Answer: Disregard for virtuous activity is pramada. It has 15 kinds: 4 vikathas (idle/harmful talk), 4 kashayas, 5 sense-objects, sleep, and attachment (sneha).

**Question 24.** How many sub-types (uttara-bheda) does asrava have?
Answer: 57. Five [types of] mithyatva, 12 avirati, 25 kashaya, 15 yoga.

**Question 25.** Why has pramada not been included here [in this count]?
Answer: Pramada has already been subsumed within kashaya.

# 17. Dhyana (Meditation)

**Question 1.** What is dhyana (meditation)?
Answer: Concentrating the mind single-pointedly on one object is dhyana. It has four kinds: arta-dhyana (sorrowful meditation), raudra-dhyana (cruel meditation), dharma-dhyana (righteous meditation), shukla-dhyana (pure meditation).

**Question 2.** Which meditations are causes of samsara (worldly bondage), and which are causes of moksha?
Answer: Arta-dhyana and raudra-dhyana are causes of samsara, while dharma-dhyana and shukla-dhyana are causes of moksha.

**Question 3.** Who can meditate, and for how long can meditation continue at most?
Answer: Only those possessed of an excellent samhanana (skeletal constitution) can meditate, and even then only for an antarmuhurta (a fraction of a muhurta) at most.

**Question 4.** What are the excellent samhananas?
Answer: Vajra-vrishabha-naracha, vajra-naracha, and naracha-samhanana.

**Question 5.** What is an antarmuhurta?
Answer: A period of forty-eight minutes is called a muhurta; the time between an avali and a full muhurta is called an antarmuhurta.

**Question 6.** What are samaya (instant) and avali?
Answer: The smallest mode/unit of kala-dravya (time) is called a samaya. An innumerable number of samayas make one avali.

**Question 7.** How many kinds of arta-dhyana are there?
Answer: Four: ishta-viyoga (separation from what is liked), anishta-samyoga (union with what is disliked), pida-chintana (brooding over pain), nidana-bandha (longing for future pleasures).

**Question 8.** What is ishta-viyoga arta-dhyana?
Answer: The brooding that occurs, seeking reunion, when one is separated from a beloved object.

**Question 9.** What is anishta-samyoga arta-dhyana?
Answer: The brooding done to find release when one is united with an undesired object.

**Question 10.** What is pida-chintana arta-dhyana?
Answer: Brooding, whenever any kind of pain occurs, over when it will end.

**Question 11.** What is nidana-bandha arta-dhyana?
Answer: Brooding with the aim of obtaining worldly pleasures in the future.

**Question 12.** What is raudra-dhyana?
Answer: Taking delight in violence and the like. It has four kinds: himsananda (delight in violence), chauryananda (delight in theft), mrishananda (delight in falsehood), and parigrahananda (delight in possessiveness).

**Question 13.** What is dharma-dhyana?
Answer: Meditation imbued with dharma (righteousness) is called dharma-dhyana. It has four kinds: 1. ajna-vichaya, 2. apaya-vichaya, 3. vipaka-vichaya, 4. sansthana-vichaya.

**Question 14.** What is ajna-vichaya dharma-dhyana, and what does it produce?
Answer: Reflecting that the scripture (agama) proclaimed by the omniscient is authoritative, and that it is exactly so and not otherwise, is ajna-vichaya dharma-dhyana. It assists firmness (nishtha) of conviction in the tattvas.

**Question 15.** What is apaya-vichaya dharma-dhyana, and what does it produce?
Answer: Reflecting on how the wrong faith (mithya-darshan) of deluded beings may be removed. This produces detachment from the world and from sensual pleasures.

**Question 16.** What is vipaka-vichaya dharma-dhyana, and what does it produce?
Answer: Reflecting on the fruits (results) of karma. This produces knowledge of the diversity of karmic fruits and their causes.

**Question 17.** What is sansthana-vichaya dharma-dhyana, and what does it produce?
Answer: Reflecting on the arrangement of the three worlds. It has four kinds — pindastha, padastha, rupastha, rupatita. It firms up one's knowledge of the arrangement of the three worlds.

**Question 18.** What is pindastha-dhyana?
Answer: Meditating on oneself by means of [prescribed] contemplations (dharanas) is pindastha-dhyana.

**Question 19.** What is padastha-dhyana?
Answer: Reflecting with the support of sacred mantras is padastha-dhyana.

**Question 20.** What is rupastha-dhyana?
Answer: Meditating on the Arihanta and other Parameshthis (supreme beings) is rupastha-dhyana.

**Question 21.** What is rupatita-dhyana?
Answer: Reflecting on the pure form of the soul is rupatita-dhyana.

**Question 22.** What is shukla-dhyana?
Answer: Pure meditation is called shukla-dhyana. It has four kinds: 1. prithaktva-vitarka, 2. ekatva-vitarka, 3. sukshma-kriya-pratipati, 4. vyuparata-kriya-nivartti.

**Question 23.** What does prithaktva-vitarka shukla-dhyana produce? In which gunasthana does it occur?
Answer: It produces the subsidence and destruction of mohaniya karma (deluding karma). It occurs from the eighth to the eleventh gunasthana.

**Question 24.** What does ekatva-vitarka shukla-dhyana produce? In which gunasthana does it occur?
Answer: It produces the destruction of jnanavarana, darshanavarana and antaraya karmas. It occurs in the twelfth gunasthana.

**Question 25.** What does sukshma-kriya-pratipati shukla-dhyana produce?
Answer: It produces the cessation of the yogas (activities), and it occurs in the thirteenth gunasthana.

**Question 26.** What does vyuparata-kriya-nivritti shukla-dhyana produce?
Answer: It produces the end of samsara, freedom from the body, and the attainment of moksha; it occurs in the fourteenth gunasthana.

**Question 27.** From which to which gunasthana does arta-dhyana occur?
Answer: From the first to the sixth gunasthana.

**Question 28.** From which to which gunasthana does raudra-dhyana occur?
Answer: From the first to the fifth gunasthana.

**Question 29.** Up to which gunasthana does dharma-dhyana continue?
Answer: From the fourth to the eighth gunasthana (according to another opinion, up to the tenth).

**Question 30.** From which to which gunasthana does shukla-dhyana occur?
Answer: From the eleventh to the fourteenth gunasthana (another opinion: from the eighth to the fourteenth).

# 18. Bhava (Psychic Disposition)

**Question 1.** What is bhava?
Answer: The disposition of the conscious being is called bhava. When qualities manifest upon the subsidence, etc., of their opposing karmas, they are called bhava.

**Question 2.** What is the material cause (upadana-karana) of the bhavas? What are they called?
Answer: They occur only within the soul. They are called the soul's own intrinsic realities (nija-tattva).

**Question 3.** How many mula-bhavas (root/basic dispositions) are there?
Answer: Five: aupashamika (subsidential), kshayika (destructional), kshayopashamika (destructional-cum-subsidential), audayika (operative), parinamika (natural/innate).

**Question 4.** What is aupashamika bhava? How many kinds are there? Name them.
Answer: The qualities that manifest when their opposing karmas subside are called aupashamika bhava. It has two kinds — aupashamika-samyaktva, aupashamika-charitra.

**Question 5.** What is aupashamika-samyaktva? How many kinds does it have?
Answer: The samyaktva that occurs through the subsidence of the four anantanubandhi kashayas and the three darshana-mohaniya natures — these seven — is aupashamika-samyaktva. It has two kinds: prathamopashama-samyaktva (first subsidential right-faith), dvitiyopashama-samyaktva (second subsidential right-faith).

**Question 6.** What is prathamopashama-samyaktva?
Answer: The subsidential-samyaktva that occurs immediately after mithyatva (wrong faith) is called prathamopashama-samyaktva.

**Question 7.** What is dvitiyopashama-samyaktva?
Answer: The subsidential-samyaktva that occurs immediately after kshayopashamika-samyaktva is called dvitiyopashama-samyaktva.

**Question 8.** What is aupashamika charitra (subsidential conduct)?
Answer: The conduct that occurs through the subsidence of the 21 natures of charitra-mohaniya (conduct-deluding karma).

**Question 9.** What is kshayika bhava?
Answer: The qualities that manifest through the total (complete) destruction of their opposing karmas.

**Question 10.** How many kinds of kshayika bhava are there? What are they?
Answer: There are 9 kinds: 1. kshayika-jnana (knowledge), 2. kshayika-darshan (perception), 3. kshayika-dana (giving), 4. kshayika-labha (gain), 5. kshayika-bhoga (one-time enjoyment), 6. kshayika-upabhoga (repeated enjoyment), 7. kshayika-virya (energy), 8. kshayika-samyaktva (right faith), 9. kshayika-charitra (conduct).

**Question 11.** What is kshayopashamika bhava? How many are there? Name them.
Answer: The dispositions that manifest through the operative-destruction (udayabhavi-kshaya) of the completely-obscuring (sarvaghati) portions of one's opposing karmas, together with the subsidence-in-potential-existence of those same portions, and the rise of the partially-obscuring (deshaghati) portions, are called kshayopashamika bhava. There are eighteen of these: four kinds of jnana (knowledge), three kinds of ajnana (wrong knowledge), three kinds of darshan (perception), five labdhis (attainments), kshayopashamika-samyaktva, kshayopashamika-charitra, and samyamasamyam (partial restraint).

**Question 12.** What is audayika bhava?
Answer: The bearing of fruit by karmas, owing to the occasioning factors of dravya (substance), kshetra (place), kala (time) and bhava (disposition), is called udaya (rise/fruition). Dispositions occasioned by such rise are called audayika bhava.

**Question 13.** How many kinds of audayika bhava are there? Name them.
Answer: There are 21: 4 gatis (destinies), 4 kashayas (passions), 3 lingas (genders), 6 leshyas (thought-colourations), mithya-darshan (wrong faith), ajnana (wrong knowledge), asamyam (lack of restraint), asiddhatva (state of non-liberation).

**Question 14.** What is parinamika bhava? How many are there?
Answer: The dispositions that occur purely by the intrinsic nature of jiva-dravya, without dependence on the rise, subsidence, destruction or destruction-cum-subsidence of karmas, are parinamika bhava. There are three: jivatva (soul-hood), bhavyatva (fitness for liberation), abhavyatva (unfitness for liberation).

**Question 15.** How many bhavas occur in the mithyatva (first) gunasthana? Name them.
Answer: 34. Ten of kshayopashamika, 21 of audayika, and 3 of parinamika.

**Question 16.** How many bhavas occur in the second and third gunasthanas?
Answer: 32 bhavas — ten of kshayopashamika, 20 of audayika (excluding mithya-darshan), and, of parinamika, jivatva and bhavyatva.

**Question 17.** How many bhavas occur in the fourth gunasthana? Which ones?
Answer: 23 bhavas — 2 of aupashamika, 2 of kshayika, 12 of kshayopashamika, 5 of audayika, and 2 of parinamika.

**Question 18.** How many bhavas occur in the fifth gunasthana? Which ones?
Answer: 31 bhavas — upashama-samyaktva, kshayika-samyaktva, 13 of kshayopashamika, 14 of audayika, and 2 of parinamika.

**Question 19.** Which bhavas occur in the sixth and seventh gunasthanas?
Answer: Upashama-samyaktva, kshayika-samyaktva, 14 of kshayopashamika, 13 of audayika, and 2 of parinamika — 31 bhavas in all.

**Question 20.** How many bhavas occur in the eighth and ninth gunasthanas? Name them.
Answer: 29 bhavas — upashama-samyaktva, upashama-charitra, kshayika-samyaktva, kshayika-charitra, 12 of kshayopashamika, 11 of audayika, and 2 of parinamika.

**Question 21.** Which bhavas occur in the tenth gunasthana?
Answer: 23 bhavas — 2 of aupashamika, 2 of kshayika, 12 of kshayopashamika, 5 of audayika, and 2 of parinamika.

**Question 22.** Which bhavas occur in the eleventh gunasthana?
Answer: 21 bhavas — 2 of aupashamika, 1 of kshayika-samyaktva, 12 of kshayopashamika, 4 of audayika, and 2 of parinamika.

**Question 23.** Which bhavas occur in the twelfth gunasthana?
Answer: 20 bhavas — 2 of kshayika, 12 of kshayopashamika, 4 of audayika, and 2 of parinamika.

**Question 24.** How many bhavas occur in the thirteenth gunasthana?
Answer: 14 bhavas — 9 of kshayika, 3 of audayika, and 2 of parinamika.

**Question 25.** How many bhavas occur in the fourteenth gunasthana?
Answer: 13 bhavas — 9 of kshayika, 2 of audayika, and 2 of parinamika.

**Question 26.** How many bhavas do the Siddhas (liberated souls) have?
Answer: 5 bhavas — kshayika-jnana, kshayika-darshan, kshayika-samyaktva, kshayika-virya, and jivatva.

# 19. Gunasthana (Stages of Spiritual Development)

**Question 1.** What is gunasthana? How many are there? Name them.
Answer: The particular gradational states of the soul's qualities of right faith (samyagdarshan), right knowledge (samyagjnana) and right conduct (samyakcharitra) — states occasioned by moha (delusion) and yoga (activity) — are called gunasthanas. There are fourteen of these: (1) mithyatva, (2) sasadana, (3) mishra, (4) avirata, (5) desha-samyama, (6) pramatta-virata, (7) apramatta-virata, (8) apurvakarana, (9) anivrittikarana, (10) sukshma-samparaya, (11) upashanta-moha, (12) kshina-moha, (13) sayoga-kevali, (14) ayoga-kevali.

**Question 2.** The first four gunasthanas are occasioned by what?
Answer: By darshana-moha (faith-deluding karma).

**Question 3.** The fifth to the twelfth gunasthanas are occasioned by what?
Answer: By charitra-moha (conduct-deluding karma).

**Question 4.** The thirteenth and fourteenth gunasthanas are occasioned by what?
Answer: By yoga (activity).

**Question 5.** What is mithyatva (the first gunasthana)?
Answer: The particular state of the soul characterised by lack of faith in the true realities (tattvas), arising from the rise of mithyatva-prakriti (wrong-faith karma), is called mithyatva.

**Question 6.** What is sasadana (the second gunasthana)?
Answer: During the period of prathamopashama-samyaktva, when at least one instant and at most six avalis remain, the state devoid of samyaktva that occurs through the rise of any one anantanubandhi-kashaya is called the sasadana gunasthana.

**Question 7.** What is the mishra (third) gunasthana?
Answer: Through the rise of samyagmithyatva-prakriti, when a soul's disposition is neither purely of the nature of samyaktva nor purely of the nature of mithyatva, but a mixture of both, this is called the mishra gunasthana.

**Question 8.** What is avirata-samyaktva (the fourth gunasthana)?
Answer: Where samyagdarshan (right faith) has arisen but, owing to the rise of apratyakhyanavarana kashaya, the vow of renouncing the five sins has not yet been taken, this is called avirata-samyaktva.

**Question 9.** What is desha-samyata (the fifth gunasthana)?
Answer: The particular disposition of the soul in which, through the non-rise of apratyakhyanavarana kashaya, there is restraint (samyam) in the form of renouncing violence against trasa (mobile) beings, but, through the rise of pratyakhyanavarana kashaya, violence against sthavara (immobile) beings is not renounced — remaining in the form of asamyam (partial non-restraint) — is called the desha-samyata gunasthana.

**Question 10.** What is the pramatta-virata (sixth) gunasthana?
Answer: The state of restraint (samyam) accompanied by carelessness (pramada), arising through the intense rise of sanjvalana kashaya and the no-kashayas, is called pramatta-virata.

**Question 11.** What is the apramatta-virata (seventh) gunasthana? How many kinds does it have?
Answer: The state of restraint free from carelessness, arising through the mild rise of sanjvalana kashaya and no-kashayas, is called apramatta-virata gunasthana. It has two kinds: svasthana-apramatta-virata and satishaya-apramatta-virata.

**Question 12.** What is svasthana-apramatta-virata gunasthana?
Answer: The disposition in which a soul continuously moves back and forth between the sixth and the seventh gunasthanas.

**Question 13.** What is satishaya-apramatta-virata?
Answer: That state in which a soul is on the verge of ascending the shreni (the ladder of spiritual purification).

**Question 14.** What is shreni (the ladder/ascending scale)?
Answer: The sequential subsidence or destruction of the 21 natures of charitra-mohaniya karma is called shreni.

**Question 15.** Which gunasthanas belong to the upashama-shreni (subsidential ladder) and the kshapaka-shreni (destructive ladder)?
Answer: The upashama-shreni comprises gunasthanas 8, 9, 10 and 11; the kshapaka-shreni comprises gunasthanas 8, 9, 10 and 12.

**Question 16.** What is the apurvakarana (eighth) gunasthana?
Answer: That state in which the soul's disposition, from moment to moment, keeps taking on ever-newer (apurva) forms — that is, souls existing at different instants always have dissimilar dispositions, while souls existing at the same instant may have dispositions that are similar or dissimilar — is called apurvakarana.

**Question 17.** What is the anivrittikarana (ninth) gunasthana?
Answer: That state in which souls existing at different instants always have dissimilar dispositions, while souls existing at the very same instant have exactly similar dispositions.

**Question 18.** What is the sukshma-samparaya (tenth) gunasthana?
Answer: The disposition of a soul experiencing the rise of extremely subtle lobha-kashaya (greed) alone is called the sukshma-samparaya gunasthana.

**Question 19.** What is upashanta-moha (the eleventh gunasthana)?
Answer: The disposition accompanied by yathakhyata-charitra (ideal conduct) that occurs through the subsidence of all 21 natures of charitra-mohaniya karma is called upashanta-moha.

**Question 20.** What is the kshina-moha (twelfth) gunasthana?
Answer: The disposition accompanied by an utterly flawless yathakhyata-charitra, occurring through the destruction of mohaniya karma, is called kshina-moha.

**Question 21.** What is the sayoga-kevali (thirteenth) gunasthana?
Answer: The disposition, free of the four ghatiya (destructive) karmas and endowed with kevala-jnana (omniscience) and yoga (activity), is called sayoga-kevali.

**Question 22.** What is the ayoga-kevali (fourteenth) gunasthana?
Answer: The disposition free of ghatiya karmas and of yoga, and endowed with the ananta-chatushtaya (the four infinite qualities), is called ayoga-kevali gunasthana.

**Question 23.** How many gunasthanas occur in deva-gati (celestial existence) and naraka-gati (hell existence)?
Answer: Gunasthanas one to four.

**Question 24.** How many gunasthanas occur in tiryancha-gati (animal/plant existence)?
Answer: Gunasthanas one to five.

**Question 25.** How many gunasthanas occur in manushya-gati (human existence)?
Answer: All fourteen, from one to fourteen.

**Question 26.** Which gunasthana applies to beings from ekendriya (one-sensed) up to asamjni-panchendriya (mindless five-sensed)?
Answer: The mithyatva gunasthana. (According to another opinion, sasadana also occurs in them during the aparyapta [undeveloped] state.)

**Question 27.** Up to which gunasthana does asamyam (lack of restraint) occur?
Answer: Up to the fourth gunasthana.

**Question 28.** Up to which gunasthana do pramada (carelessness) and deliberate attachment occur?
Answer: Up to the sixth gunasthana.

**Question 29.** Up to which gunasthana does kashaya (passion) occur?
Answer: Up to the tenth gunasthana.

**Question 30.** Up to which gunasthana do yoga and the operation (udaya) of the body [-determining karma] occur?
Answer: Up to the thirteenth gunasthana.

**Question 31.** Up to which gunasthana does veda (sex-disposition) occur?
Answer: All three psychic genders (bhava-veda) last up to the ninth gunasthana.

**Question 32.** How many yogas occur in the first and second gunasthanas?
Answer: Thirteen yogas — all except aharaka and aharaka-mishra.

**Question 33.** How many yogas occur in the third gunasthana? Which ones?
Answer: 10 yogas — the four mental, the four vocal, and the audarika and vaikriyika bodily yogas.

**Question 34.** How many yogas occur in the fifth gunasthana and from the seventh up to the twelfth gunasthana?
Answer: Nine yogas — the four mental, the four vocal, and the audarika bodily yoga.

**Question 35.** How many yogas occur in the sixth gunasthana?
Answer: Eleven yogas in the sixth gunasthana — the four mental, the four vocal, and the audarika, aharaka and aharaka-mishra bodily yogas.

**Question 36.** How many yogas occur in the thirteenth gunasthana?
Answer: Seven — true-mental, neither-true-nor-untrue-mental, true-vocal, neither-true-nor-untrue-vocal, audarika, audarika-mishra, and karmana yoga.

**Question 37.** How long is the duration (kala) of the first gunasthana?
Answer: Anadi-anant (beginningless-and-endless), anadi-sant (beginningless-but-with-an-end), or sadi-sant (with-a-beginning-and-with-an-end) — of three kinds.
