# 1. Namokar Mantra

**Question 1.** Write the Namokar Mantra.
Answer: Namo Arihantanam, Namo Siddhanam, Namo Airiyanam, Namo Uvajjhayanam, Namo Loe Savva-sahunam.

**Question 2.** Write the greatness (mahatmya) of the Namokar Mantra, along with its meaning.
Answer: Eso Pancha Namokaro, Savva Pavappanasano; Mangalanam Cha Savvesim, Padhamam Hoi Mangalam.
(Translation: This five-fold obeisance destroys all sin; and among all auspicious things, it is the very first auspicious thing.) This five-fold obeisance mantra — the Namokar Mantra — destroys all the sins committed by us, and it is the foremost among all auspicious things (mangalas).

**Question 3.** Who composed the Namokar Mantra?
Answer: In terms of its underlying meaning (bhava), the Namokar Mantra is beginningless and endless (anadi-nidhan, without origin or end). No one composed it.

**Question 4.** Who first wrote it down, and where?
Answer: It was first written down as the mangalacharan (opening auspicious invocation) of the Shatkhandagama, by Acharya Shri Pushpadanta ji and Bhutabali ji.

**Question 5.** In which language, and in which meter, is the Namokar Mantra composed?
Answer: It is in the Prakrit language, in the gatha meter.

**Question 6.** Give the meaning of the Namokar Mantra.
Answer: Obeisance to the Arihants (Arihant, the Worthy Victor who has destroyed the four destructive/ghati karmas). Obeisance to the Siddhas (Siddha, the fully Liberated Souls). Obeisance to the Acharyas (Acharya, the head-preceptors of the monastic order). Obeisance to the Upadhyayas (Upadhyaya, the teacher-preceptors). Obeisance to all the Sadhus (Sadhu, monks) that exist anywhere in the world.

**Question 7.** What other readings of "Arihant" are found?
Answer: In the Sapta Smarana (the Seven Recollections) three readings are given — (1) Arahantanam (2) Arihantanam (3) Aruhantanam. Ancient handwritten manuscripts also contain the reading "Ahamtanam."

**Question 8.** Are the other readings incorrect?
Answer: No — in all of them the word is derived from "Arhat" (the Worthy One). Hence all of them are correct.

**Question 9.** What is the meaning of "Aruhantanam"?
Answer: One who is not reborn. That is, because the seed of karma has been burnt away, those for whom rebirth no longer occurs are called Aruhantanam.

**Question 10.** How is "Arahantanam" a correct reading?
Answer: Because they are supremely worshipped by the gods (devas), they are called Arahant or Arhant.

**Question 11.** What is the meaning of "Arihantanam"?
Answer: "Ari" means enemy. Hence those who have destroyed (kshaya) the enemy in the form of karma — the ghati (destructive) karmas — are called Arihant.

**Question 12.** Explain how the syllable Om (ॐ) is contained within the Namokar Mantra.
Answer: The "अ" (a) of Arihant, the "अ" (a) of Siddha (the bodiless one), the "आ" (aa) of Acharya, the "उ" (u) of Upadhyaya, and the "म" (m) of Muni [combine as follows]: a + a = aa; aa + u = o; o + m = Om (ॐ).

**Question 13.** To whom is obeisance offered in the Namokar Mantra?
Answer: In the Namokar Mantra, obeisance is offered to the Panch Parameshthi (the Five Supreme Beings).

**Question 14.** Who are the Panch Parameshthi?
Answer: Arihant, Siddha, Acharya, Upadhyaya, and all Sadhus.

**Question 15.** Who is called a Parameshthi?
Answer: One who is established in the supreme state (param pad) is called a Parameshthi (one abiding in the Supreme Station).

**Question 16.** Who is called Arihant Parameshthi?
Answer: Those who have destroyed the four ghati (destructive) karmas and who have attained the Ananta Chatushtaya (the Four Infinitudes) are called Arihant.

**Question 17.** Who is called Siddha Parameshthi?
Answer: Those who have become forever free of dravya karma (material karma), bhava karma (psychic karma), and no-karma (the body and other quasi-karmic matter), and who have attained the wealth of liberation (moksha-lakshmi), are called Siddha Parameshthi.

**Question 18.** Who is called Acharya Parameshthi?
Answer: Those who bear the 36 fundamental virtues (mula-gunas) and who direct the order of monks (muni-sangha), and who confer initiation (diksha), instruction (shiksha), and atonement/penance (prayashchitta).

**Question 19.** Who is called Upadhyaya Parameshthi?
Answer: Those who study and teach the scriptures within the order of monks.

**Question 20.** Who is called Sadhu Parameshthi?
Answer: Those who remain constantly absorbed in knowledge, meditation, and austerity (tapa).

**Question 21.** How many fundamental virtues (mula-guna) does the Arihant Parameshthi have? List them separately.
Answer: There are 46 fundamental virtues — 36 marvels (atishaya), comprising 10 of birth, 10 of Kevalgyan (omniscience), and 14 wrought by the gods; together with the 8 divine accompaniments (pratiharya); and the 4 infinitudes (ananta chatushtaya).

**Question 22.** Name the ten marvels (atishaya) of birth.
Answer: 1. the absence of urine and excrement, 2. white blood, 3. a beautiful form, 4. incomparable strength, 5. a perfectly symmetrical body (samachaturasra-samsthana), 6. the adamantine bone-and-joint structure (vajra-vrishabha-naracha-samhanan), 7. speech that is beneficial, measured, and pleasant, 8. a naturally fragrant body, 9. a body free of fatigue, 10. one thousand and eight auspicious marks on the body.

**Question 23.** Name the ten marvels (atishaya) of Kevalgyan (omniscience).
Answer: (1) abundant food within a hundred yojanas in all four directions, (2) the power to move through the sky, (3) no longer needing to take mouthfuls of food, (4) freedom from all adversity/affliction (upsarga), (5) the face appearing visible from all four directions, (6) the nails and hair no longer growing, (7) a shadowless body, (8) the eyelids no longer blinking, (9) no harm coming to any living being nearby (a state of compassion/non-violence), (10) mastery over all forms of knowledge and skill.

**Question 24.** Name the 14 marvels wrought by the gods (devakrita atishaya).
Answer: 1. all beings understanding [the divine speech] in their own language, 2. a spirit of universal amity among all, 3. the fruits and flowers of every season blossoming together, 4. the ground becoming mirror-smooth and level, 5. a gentle, fragrant breeze, 6. the absence of plague and calamity (a state of universal supreme bliss), 7. the ground becoming free of dust and thorns, 8. a shower of scented water, 9. a clear, cloudless sky, 10. golden lotuses arising beneath each footstep, 11. the earth becoming filled with wealth and grain, 12. the Dharma-chakra (wheel of the Law) moving before him, 13. cries of victory resounding in the sky, 14. the eight auspicious substances (ashta-mangala-dravya).

**Question 25.** What are the eight divine accompaniments (ashta-pratiharya)?
Answer: 1. the Ashoka tree, 2. a rain of celestial flowers, 3. the lion-throne (simhasana), 4. the sixty-four fly-whisks (chamara), 5. the triple canopy (three chhatra), 6. the divine sound/speech (divya-dhvani), 7. the divine kettle-drums (devadundubhi), 8. the halo of light (prabhamandala).

**Question 26.** What are the four infinitudes (ananta chatushtaya)?
Answer: 1. Anantajnana (infinite knowledge) 2. Anantadarshan (infinite perception) 3. Anantasukha (infinite bliss) 4. Anantavirya (infinite power).

**Question 27.** How many of the Arihant Parameshthi's virtues belong to the soul, and how many belong to the body?
Answer: The four infinitudes belong to the soul, and the remaining 42 virtues are body-based.

**Question 28.** Through the absence of which karmas do the four infinitudes manifest?
Answer: Through the destruction of jnanavaraniya karma (knowledge-obscuring karma), infinite knowledge; through the destruction of darshanavaraniya karma (perception-obscuring karma), infinite perception; through the destruction of mohaniya karma (deluding karma), infinite bliss; through the destruction of antaraya karma (obstructing karma), infinite power.

**Question 29.** How many karmic states does the Arihant destroy?
Answer: 63 subsidiary karmic states (uttara-prakriti).

**Question 30.** What are these 63 states?
Answer: 47 belonging to the ghati (destructive) karmas, 13 belonging to nama karma (body-determining karma), and 3 belonging to ayu karma (life-span karma).

**Question 31.** What are the 13 states of nama karma?
Answer: hell-destiny, the transmigratory-link to hell-destiny, animal-destiny, the transmigratory-link to animal-destiny, the "three incompletenesses" (viklatraya: two-sensed, three-sensed, and four-sensed embodiment), one-sensed embodiment, bioluminescence (udyota), heat-body (atapa), subtle body (sukshma), common/undifferentiated body (sadharana), and immobility (sthavara).

**Question 32.** Which three life-spans (ayu) does an Arihant never have?
Answer: hell-life-span, animal-life-span, and celestial-life-span.

**Question 33.** How many fundamental virtues does the Siddha Parameshthi have? Name them.
Answer: There are eight fundamental virtues — infinite knowledge, infinite perception, the destruction-based right faith (kshayika samyaktva), infinite power, the state of occupying a fixed measure of space (avagahanatva), subtleness (sukshmatva), imperturbability (avyabadhatva), and weightless-buoyancy (agurulaghutva).

**Question 34.** Through the absence of which karmas do the Siddha's eight virtues manifest?
Answer: Through jnanavaraniya karma, infinite knowledge; through darshanavaraniya karma, infinite perception; through mohaniya karma, destruction-based right faith; through antaraya karma, infinite power; through nama karma, subtleness; through ayu karma, the fixed-space-occupying quality; through gotra karma (status-determining karma), weightless-buoyancy; and through vedaniya karma (feeling-producing karma), imperturbability.

**Question 35.** How many fundamental virtues does the Acharya Parameshthi have? What are they?
Answer: The Acharya Parameshthi has 36 fundamental virtues — 10 dharmas (virtues), 12 austerities (tapa), the 5 branches of right conduct (panchachara), the 6 essential duties (avashyaka), and the 3 restraints (gupti).

**Question 36.** Name the ten dharmas (dasha-dharma).
Answer: supreme forgiveness (uttama-kshama), humility (mardava), straightforwardness (arjava), truthfulness (satya), contentment/purity (shaucha), self-restraint (sanyama), austerity (tapa), renunciation (tyaga), non-attachment (akinchanya), and celibacy (brahmacharya).

**Question 37.** What are the twelve austerities (barah tapa)?
Answer: six external (bahya) and six internal (abhyantar).

**Question 38.** What are the six external austerities?
Answer: fasting (anashan), reduced diet (avamaudarya), restriction of the range of alms accepted (vritti-parisankhyana), dwelling in a solitary spot (vivikta-shayyasana), giving up rich or flavorful food (rasa-parityaga), and mortification of the body (kayaklesha).

**Question 39.** What are the six internal austerities?
Answer: atonement (prayashchitta), reverence (vinaya), service to fellow ascetics (vaiyavritya), scriptural study (svadhyaya), the letting-go of attachment to the body (vyutsarga), and meditation (dhyana).

**Question 40.** What are the five branches of right conduct (panchachara)?
Answer: right-faith conduct (darshanachara), right-knowledge conduct (jnanachara), right-character conduct (charitrachara), austerity conduct (tapachara), and energy conduct (viryachara).

**Question 41.** What are the six essential duties (avashyaka) of an Acharya?
Answer: equanimity practice (samayika), praise of the twenty-four Tirthankaras (chaturvimshati-stavana), veneration (vandana), repentance (pratikramana), the vow of renunciation (pratyakhyana), and bodily abandonment in meditation (kayotsarga).

**Question 42.** What are the three restraints (gupti)?
Answer: restraint of mind (mano-gupti), restraint of speech (vachana-gupti), and restraint of body (kaya-gupti).

**Question 43.** How many fundamental virtues does the Upadhyaya Parameshthi have?
Answer: 25 fundamental virtues — the eleven Angas (primary scriptural limbs) and the fourteen Purvas (ancient scriptural texts).

**Question 44.** How many fundamental virtues does the Sadhu Parameshthi have?
Answer: 28 fundamental virtues — the five great vows (mahavrata), the five carefulnesses (samiti), the restraint of the five senses, the six essential duties, and seven other remaining virtues.

**Question 45.** What are the five great vows (panch mahavrata)?
Answer: non-violence (ahimsa), truthfulness (satya), non-stealing (achaurya), celibacy (brahmacharya), and non-possession (aparigraha).

**Question 46.** Name the five carefulnesses (samiti).
Answer: care in walking (irya), care in speech (bhasha), care in seeking food (eshana), care in picking up and setting down objects (adana-nikshepana), and care in the disposal of waste (pratishthapana).

**Question 47.** What are the other seven virtues?
Answer: plucking out one's own hair (keshalunchana), not bathing (asnana), sleeping on the bare ground (bhumishayana), not brushing the teeth [with any instrument] (adantadhavana), eating while standing (khade-khade bhojana), nakedness (nagnatva), and eating only once a day.

**Question 48.** How many syllables does the Namokar Mantra have?
Answer: 35 syllables — Namo Arihantanam: 7, Namo Siddhanam: 5, Namo Airiyanam: 7, Namo Uvajjhayanam: 7, Namo Loe Savva-sahunam: 9. (7+5+7+7+9=35.)

**Question 49.** How many lines (pada) does the Namokar Mantra have?
Answer: Five lines — 1. Namo Arihantanam, 2. Namo Siddhanam, 3. Namo Airiyanam, 4. Namo Uvajjhayanam, 5. Namo Loe Savva-sahunam.

**Question 50.** Which Parameshthis can possess the four kinds of knowledge?
Answer: Three Parameshthis — Acharya, Upadhyaya, and Sadhu.

**Question 51.** Which Parameshthi is certain to attain liberation in this very life?
Answer: The Arihant Parameshthi alone.

**Question 52.** Which Parameshthis make use of the senses and the mind, and engage in deliberative thought?
Answer: Three Parameshthis — Acharya, Upadhyaya, and Sadhu Parameshthi.

**Question 53.** Which Parameshthis are free of the passions (kashaya)?
Answer: Arihant and Siddha Parameshthi.

**Question 54.** Which Parameshthis are free of both karma and body?
Answer: The Siddha Parameshthi alone.

**Question 55.** Which Parameshthis practise the plucking out of hair (keshalunchana)?
Answer: Acharya, Upadhyaya, and Sadhu — three.

**Question 56.** Which Parameshthis wander [from place to place]?
Answer: Arihant, Acharya, Upadhyaya, and Sadhu — these four Parameshthis wander.

**Question 57.** Which Parameshthis do not keep a pichi (peacock-feather whisk) and kamandalu (water-pot)?
Answer: Two Parameshthis — Arihant and Siddha.

**Question 58.** How many syllables of the Panch Parameshthi's names can be used for japa (recitation) and dhyana (meditation)?
Answer: Thirty-five, sixteen, six, five, four, two, and one syllable can be used for japa and dhyana.

**Question 59.** What is the sixteen-syllable mantra?
Answer: Arihanta-Siddha-Airiya-Uvajjhaya-Sahu, or: Namah Arhat-Siddhacharya-Upadhyaya-Sarva-Sadhubhyah.
(Translation: Obeisance to the Arihant, the Siddha, the Acharya, the Upadhyaya, and to all the Sadhus.)

**Question 60.** What is the six-syllable mantra?
Answer: Arihant, Siddha. Arihant Sisa [Siddha]. Om Namah Siddhebhyah. Namo'rha Siddhebhyah.
(Translation: Om, obeisance to the Siddhas; obeisance to the Worthy Siddhas.)

**Question 61.** What are the five-syllable mantras?
Answer: A Si Aa U Sa [the seed-syllables for Arihant-Siddha-Acharya-Upadhyaya-Sadhu]. Namo Siddhanam (Obeisance to the Siddhas).

**Question 62.** What are the four-syllable mantras?
Answer: Arihant. A Si Sahum.

**Question 63.** What is the seven-syllable mantra?
Answer: Om Hrim Shrim Arha Namah. A Si Aa U Sa Namah.

**Question 64.** What is the eight-syllable mantra?
Answer: Om Namo Arihantanam (Om, obeisance to the Arihants).

**Question 65.** What is the two-syllable mantra?
Answer: Om Hrim. Siddha. A Si.

**Question 66.** What is the one-syllable mantra?
Answer: Om. Hrim.

**Question 67.** What is the thirteen-syllable mantra?
Answer: (1) Om Arhat Siddha Sayoga-Kevali Svaha. (2) Om Haam Hreem Hoom Haum Hah A Si Aa U Sa Namah.

**Question 68.** How many kinds of Kevali (omniscient beings) are there? What are they?
Answer: Seven kinds — (1) Tirthankara-Kevali (2) Samanya-Kevali (ordinary Kevali) (3) Upasarga-Kevali (afflicted Kevali) (4) Muka-Kevali (silent Kevali) (5) Antakrit-Kevali (Kevali who attains liberation at the very moment of omniscience) (6) Samudghata-Kevali (Kevali who performs the karmic dispersion) (7) Anubaddha-Kevali (Kevali who arises in unbroken succession).

**Question 69.** Give the definition of each kind of Kevali.
Answer:
1. **Tirthankara-Kevali** — one in whom the tirthankara-nama-karma (the karma of Ford-Making) comes to fruition; whose preaching hall (samavasarana) is built; who has [the remaining] auspicious life-events (kalyanaka); and from whose presence the divine sound (divya-dhvani) issues.

2. **Samanya Kevali (ordinary Kevali)** — one who becomes a Kevali simply by destroying the four ghati (destructive) karmas, without such special marks; for whom a fragrance-hall (gandha-kuti) is built, and who also delivers religious discourse — for example, Rama and Hanuman.

3. **Upasarga-Kevali** — one to whom omniscience (kevalgyan) arises in the midst of an affliction (upasarga), and who also delivers religious discourse — for example, Kulabhushana and Deshabhushana.

4. **Muka-Kevali (silent Kevali)** — one whose speech does not resound (i.e. who does not give discourse) even after attaining omniscience.

5. **Antakrit-Kevali** — one to whom omniscience arises either through a terrible affliction or in the ordinary course, and who, at that very moment, destroys all eight karmas and attains liberation — for example, the three Pandavas.

6. **Samudghata-Kevali** — when the duration of the nama (body-determining), gotra (status-determining), and vedaniya (feeling-producing) karmas exceeds that of the ayu (life-span) karma, a Sayoga-Kevali (an omniscient being still possessing activity) performs the karmic dispersion (samudghata) at the final moment in order to equalize their duration with that of the ayu karma; such a one is called a Samudghata-Kevali.

7. **Anubaddha-Kevali** — those Kevalis who arise in unbroken succession, such that as soon as one attains liberation, the next attains omniscience, and when that one in turn attains liberation, a third attains omniscience, and so on in sequence.

# 2. Tirthankara

**Question 1.** Who is called a Tirthankara?
Answer: 1. One who sets the ford of religion (dharma-tirtha) in motion. 2. One who crosses [the ocean of worldly existence] himself and shows others the path to cross the ocean of transmigration. 3. One whose auspicious life-events (kalyanaka) occur, and whose divine preaching-hall (samavasarana) is built. 4. One who gives the true teaching of religion — such a one is called a Tirthankara.

**Question 3.** How many chief disciples (ganadhara) did all twenty-four Tirthankaras have in total?
Answer: 1,459 ganadharas.

**Question 4.** Why, when, and how — and by whom — is a Tirthankara's emblem determined?
Answer: A Tirthankara is identified by his emblem. At the time of the birth-kalyanaka (auspicious birth-event), atop Mount Sumeru, the emblem that appears on the right big toe of Lord [the infant Tirthankara] as [placed before] Saudharma Indra determines the emblem accordingly.

**Question 5.** What is meant by a kalyanaka (auspicious event)? How many are there? Name them.
Answer: The occasions on which the gods hold celebrations for the sake of a Tirthankara are called kalyanaka. There are five: 1. conception (garbha) 2. birth (janma) 3. renunciation/austerity (tapa) 4. omniscience (kevalgyan) 5. liberation (moksha).

**Question 6.** Is it necessary that every Tirthankara have all five kalyanakas?
Answer: The Tirthankaras of the Bharat and Airavat regions always have all five kalyanakas. But the Tirthankaras of the Videha region may have two, three, or five kalyanakas.

**Question 7.** For whom are there [only] two kalyanakas?
Answer: For one who first takes renunciation (diksha) and only afterward binds the tirthankara-nama-karma, and who attains liberation in that same life — for such a one, only the omniscience (jnana) and liberation (moksha) kalyanakas occur.

**Question 8.** How would there be three kalyanakas?
Answer: For one who will attain liberation in that same life, but who did not take renunciation [before binding the tirthankara-karma] — having bound the tirthankara-nama-karma in this very life — his kalyanakas will be renunciation (tapa), omniscience (jnana), and liberation (moksha).

**Question 9.** At most, how many Tirthankaras can there be at one and the same time?
Answer: At any one time, there can be at most 170 Tirthankaras.

**Question 10.** How can there be 170 at once?
Answer: In relation to one Mount Sumeru there are one Bharat, one Airavat, and 32 Videha regions [each capable of having a Tirthankara]. In this way, across the five Merus, 34 × 5 = 170 Tirthankaras can exist.

**Question 11.** At the very least, how many Tirthankaras can there be at one time?
Answer: There are [at least] 20 Tirthankaras.

**Question 12.** Are there Tirthankaras existing right now? If so, where?
Answer: Yes — at present, there are 20 Tirthankaras in the Videha regions belonging to the five Merus.

**Question 13.** At present, how many Tirthankaras are there in Jambudvipa? Name them.
Answer: Simandhar, Yugamandhar, Bahu, and Subahu are the four Tirthankaras.

**Question 14.** In Dhatakikhanda, which Tirthankaras belong to Vijaya-meru?
Answer: Sanjata, Svayamprabha, Rishabhanana, and Anantavirya — these four.

**Question 15.** In Dhatakikhanda, which Tirthankaras belong to Achala-meru?
Answer: Suriprabha, Vishalkirti, Vajradhar, and Chandranana — these four.

**Question 16.** In Pushkarardha-dvipa, which Tirthankaras belong to Mandara-meru?
Answer: Bhadrabahu, Bhujangam, Ishwar, and Nemiprabh — these four.

**Question 17.** In Pushkarardha-dvipa, name the Tirthankaras belonging to Vidyunmali-meru.
Answer: Virasen, Mahabhadra, Devayash, and Ajitavirya — these four.

**Question 18.** How many Tirthankaras of the present set of twenty-four observed lifelong celibacy from childhood (bal-brahmachari)? Name them.
Answer: Five Tirthankaras — Vasupujya, Mallinath, Neminath, Parshvanath, and Lord Mahavir.

**Question 19.** How many Tirthankaras held three offices/stations? Name them.
Answer: Three Tirthankaras — Shantinath, Kunthunath, and Aranath [who were also Chakravartis, universal emperors, in addition to being Tirthankaras].

**Question 20.** How many Tirthankaras had an affliction (upasarga) inflicted upon them?
Answer: Three Tirthankaras — Suparshvanath, Parshvanath, and Lord Mahavir.

**Question 21.** After taking renunciation, which Tirthankara attained omniscience in the shortest time?
Answer: Mallinath ji, six days after taking renunciation.

**Question 22.** After taking renunciation, which Tirthankara attained omniscience after the longest time?
Answer: Lord Adinath, one thousand years after taking renunciation.

**Question 23.** By how many, and which, Indras is the Lord Arihant worshipped?
Answer: He is venerated by 100 Indras — 40 of the Bhavanavasi [demigods], 32 of the Vyantaras, 24 of the Kalpavasis, 2 of the Jyotishas (the sun and moon), and among humans, the Chakravarti [universal emperor], and among animals, the lion. (40+32+24+2+1+1=100.)

**Question 24.** In which time-period (kala) do the 24 Tirthankaras of the Bharat and Airavat regions occur?
Answer: In the fourth time-period, Dushama-Sushama.

**Question 25.** Did the ford of religion (dharma-tirtha) continue unbroken throughout the fourth time-period, or was there ever a break?
Answer: There were breaks (vyucchitti) in the tirtha-period between seven Tirthankaras, from Pushpadanta to Dharmanath.

**Question 26.** How long was the break during the era of each of these Tirthankaras?
Answer: In Shri Pushpadanta ji's era, a quarter of a palya; in Shri Shitalnath ji's era, half a palya; in Shri Shreyansanath ji's era, three-quarters of a palya; in Shri Vasupujya ji's era, a full palya; in Shri Vimalnath ji's era, three-quarters of a palya; in Shri Anantanath ji's era, half a palya; and in Shri Dharmanath ji's era, a quarter of a palya. (A palya is an immense unit of time.)

**Question 27.** Did all the Tirthankaras appear only in the fourth time-period?
Answer: No — Lord Adinath was born in the third time-period, and attained liberation in the third time-period itself.

# 3. Deva-Darshan and Chaityalaya (Temple Visit and the Jina-Temple)

**Question 1.** Why do we take the darshan (auspicious sight) of the Lord?
Answer: The Jinendra Deva (the Victorious Lord) is free of the eighteen faults and possesses infinite virtues. We take his darshan in order to attain those virtues, and through it right faith (samyagdarshan) is also attained.

**Question 2.** Why is it necessary to bathe before coming to the temple?
Answer: In household life, there are constantly five sources of subtle violence (pancha-suna) — the grindstone, the hearth, the mortar, the broom, and the place for filtering/discarding water — through which the body becomes impure. Hence bathing is necessary to purify the body.

**Question 3.** Why should the feet be washed on entering the temple?
Answer: The feet are directly connected to the mind. If the feet remain unclean, the mind too will remain unclean, and our dirty feet would also make the temple impure. Therefore one should wash the feet before entering the temple.

**Question 4.** Why is rice specifically offered in the temple?
Answer: Because once the husk is removed, a grain of rice cannot sprout again; hence [we offer rice as a wish] that hereafter I too may not sprout again — that is, may not be reborn.

**Question 5.** To whom is circumambulation (pradakshina) offered, and how many times?
Answer: Three circumambulations are offered — to the passion-free Lords (vitaraga-deva), to the tirtha-kshetras (sites of the fords), to the siddha-kshetras (sites of liberation), to the atishaya-kshetras (sites of miraculous power), and to the unattached teachers — the munis, gurus, and aryikas (nuns).

**Question 6.** On entering the temple, why should one say "Nissahi, Nissahi"?
Answer: If some deity (unseen) or some lay devotee is engaged in darshan there, may they grant me a place to also take darshan.

**Question 7.** On leaving, why does one say "Assahi, Assahi"?
Answer: [To convey:] I have finished taking darshan and am now leaving; may whoever gave me this place now reclaim their place.

**Question 8.** Why is a bell rung in the temple?
Answer: Its auspicious sound removes our mental pollution.

**Question 9.** What is the benefit of ringing the bell?
Answer: The sound of the conch and the bell destroys disease-causing germs. In Africa, the bell is rung to draw out the poison after a venomous snake-bite; in Moscow, tuberculosis is treated by ringing a bell. (As stated in the original text.)

**Question 10.** With how many substances is puja (worship) performed? Name them.
Answer: Puja is performed with the eight substances (ashta-dravya) — water, sandalwood paste, unbroken rice grains (akshata), flowers, food-offering (naivedya), lamp (deepa), incense (dhoopa), and fruit.

**Question 11.** Why should puja be performed?
Answer: Puja is the foremost essential duty of a householder.

**Question 12.** How many limbs (anga) does puja have?
Answer: It has six limbs — abhisheka (ceremonial bathing of the image), ahvanana (invocation), sthapana (establishing [the deity's presence]), sannidhikarana (drawing near), worship with the eight substances, and visarjana (taking leave).

**Question 13.** How should one go to the temple?
Answer: One should never go to the temple empty-handed; one should take along some fruit and articles for worship.

**Question 14.** What is meant by a chaityalaya (Jina-temple)?
Answer: A place in which an image of the Jina (jina-bimba) is enshrined is called a chaityalaya.

**Question 15.** What is meant by an akritrim chaityalaya (a "not man-made," naturally-arisen temple)?
Answer: One that has not been built by any deity, vidyadhara (demigod-sorcerer), or human being; that has existed without beginning; that will remain in that same state for endless time; and in which an image of the Jina is enshrined.

**Question 16.** How many akritrim chaityalayas are there?
Answer: Eight crore, fifty-six lakh, ninety-seven thousand, four hundred and eighty-one (8,56,97,481).

**Question 17.** Is this an exact and fixed number, or can it vary?
Answer: Besides these, there are countless (asankhyata) akritrim chaityalayas belonging to the Vyantara and Jyotisha deities.

**Question 18.** How large, and how many, are the images within a single akritrim chaityalaya?
Answer: In each chaityalaya there are 108 images, each 500 dhanush (bow-lengths) tall.

**Question 19.** How many images are there altogether in the akritrim chaityalayas of the three lokas (worlds)?
Answer: 9,25,53,27,948 — nine hundred twenty-five crore, fifty-three lakh, twenty-seven thousand, nine hundred and forty-eight.

**Question 20.** How many akritrim chaityalayas are there in the lower world (adholoka), and where are they?
Answer: In the abodes of the Bhavanavasi deities there are seven crore, seventy-two lakh.

**Question 21.** How many kinds of Bhavanavasi deities are there? Name them.
Answer: There are ten kinds — Asurakumara, Nagakumara, Hemakumara (Suparna), Vidyutkumara, Agnikumara, Meghakumara (Stanita), Udadhikumara, Dvipakumara, Dikkumara, and Vayukumara.

**Question 22.** How many abodes and chaityalayas do the Asurakumara deities have?
Answer: Sixty-four lakh (64 lakh).

**Question 23.** How many abodes and chaityalayas do the Nagakumara deities have?
Answer: Eighty-four lakh (84 lakh).

**Question 24.** How many abodes and akritrim chaityalayas do the Hemakumara (Suparna) deities have?
Answer: Seventy-two lakh (72 lakh).

**Question 25.** How many abodes and akritrim chaityalayas do the Vayukumara deities have?
Answer: Ninety-six lakh (96 lakh).

**Question 26.** How many abodes and chaityalayas do the Vidyutkumara, Agnikumara, Meghakumara (Stanita), Udadhikumara, Dvipakumara, and Dikkumara deities have?
Answer: Each of these six kinds of deities has 76 lakh abodes and akritrim chaityalayas. Thus 76 lakh × 6 = four crore, fifty-six lakh.

**Question 27.** How many akritrim chaityalayas are there in the middle world (madhyaloka), and where are they?
Answer: Four hundred and fifty-eight (458). Of these, seventy-eight are in Jambudvipa, one hundred fifty-eight in Dhatakikhanda, one hundred fifty-eight in Pushkarardha-dvipa, four on Manushottar mountain, fifty-two in Nandishvara-dvipa, four in Kundalavara-dvipa, and four in Ruchakavara-dvipa; thus 78+158+158+4+52+4+4, all together making 458 akritrim chaityalayas.

**Question 28.** Where exactly are the seventy-eight chaityalayas in Jambudvipa?
Answer: In Jambudvipa, on Mount Sudarshana-meru there are 16, on the elephant-tusk mountains (gajadanta) 4, on the Jambu and Shalmali trees 2, on the Vijayardha mountains of the Videha region 32, on the Vakshara mountains 16, on the [boundary] Kulachala mountains 6, and on the Vijayardha mountains of Bharat and Airavat 2 — thus 16+4+2+32+16+6+2, in all 78 akritrim chaityalayas.

**Question 29.** How are the 458 chaityalayas of the middle world made up? Explain.
Answer: 80 on the five Merus, 20 on the elephant-tusk mountains, 10 on trees such as the Shalmali and Jambu, 170 on the Vijayardha mountains, 80 on the Vakshara mountains, 30 on the Kulachala mountains, 4 on the Ishvakara mountains, 4 on Manushottar mountain, 52 in Nandishvara-dvipa, 4 on Kundala mountain, and 4 in Ruchakavara — 458 in total.

**Question 30.** How many akritrim chaityalayas are there in the upper world (urdhvaloka)?
Answer: Eighty-four lakh, ninety-seven thousand and twenty-three (84,97,023).

**Question 31.** How many akritrim chaityalayas are there in the Saudharma heaven?
Answer: Thirty-two lakh (32 lakh).

**Question 32.** How many akritrim chaityalayas are there in the Aishana heaven?
Answer: Twenty-eight lakh (28 lakh).

**Question 33.** How many akritrim chaityalayas are there in the Sanatkumara heaven?
Answer: Twelve lakh (12 lakh).

**Question 34.** How many akritrim chaityalayas are there in the Mahendra heaven?
Answer: Eight lakh (8 lakh).

**Question 35.** How many akritrim chaityalayas are there in the Brahma-Brahmottara heaven?
Answer: Four lakh (4 lakh).

**Question 36.** How many akritrim chaityalayas are there in the Lantava-Kapishtha heaven?
Answer: Fifty thousand (50 thousand).

**Question 37.** How many akritrim chaityalayas are there in the Shukra-Mahashukra heaven?
Answer: Forty thousand (40 thousand).

**Question 38.** How many akritrim chaityalayas are there in the Satara-Sahasrara heaven?
Answer: Six thousand (6 thousand).

**Question 39.** How many akritrim chaityalayas are there in the four heavens of Anata, Pranata, Arana, and Achyuta?
Answer: Seven hundred (700).

**Question 40.** How many akritrim chaityalayas are there in the three lower Graiveyaka (Adho-Graiveyaka) heavens?
Answer: One hundred and eleven (111).

**Question 41.** How many akritrim chaityalayas are there in the three middle Graiveyaka (Madhya-Graiveyaka) heavens?
Answer: One hundred and seven (107).

**Question 42.** How many akritrim chaityalayas are there in the three upper Graiveyaka (Urdhva-Graiveyaka) heavens?
Answer: Ninety-one (91).

**Question 43.** How many akritrim chaityalayas are there in the nine Anudisha [heavens]?
Answer: Nine (9).

**Question 44.** How many akritrim chaityalayas are there in the five Anuttara [heavens]?
Answer: Five (5).

# 4. Puja (Worship)

**Question 45.** What is meant by puja (worship)?
Answer: The recitation of the virtues (gunanuvada) of Deva (the Lord), Shastra (the scripture), and Guru (the teacher) is called puja.

**Question 46.** Of whom is puja performed?
Answer: Puja is performed of Deva, Shastra, and Guru, and of the nine categories of divinities (nava-devata).

**Question 47.** How many kinds of puja are there? Name them.
Answer: Puja is of two kinds — dravya-puja (material worship) and bhava-puja (mental/inner worship).

**Question 48.** What is meant by bhava-puja?
Answer: Reciting the virtues of the Lord without the use of material substances is bhava-puja.

**Question 49.** Which mantra is recited while offering water?
Answer: Om Hrim ... Janma-Jara-Mrityu-Vinashanaya Jalam Nirvapamiti Svaha.
(Translation: Om Hrim, I offer this water for the destruction of birth, old age, and death, svaha.)

**Question 50.** Which mantra is recited while offering sandalwood paste?
Answer: Om Hrim ... Samsara-Tapa-Vinashanaya Chandanam Nirvapamiti Svaha.
(Translation: I offer this sandalwood paste for the destruction of the suffering of worldly existence, svaha.)

**Question 51.** Which mantra is recited while offering unbroken rice (akshata)?
Answer: Om Hrim ... Akshaya-Pada-Praptaye Akshatam Nirvapamiti Svaha.
(Translation: I offer this unbroken rice for the attainment of the imperishable state [of liberation], svaha.)

**Question 52.** Which mantra is recited while offering flowers?
Answer: Om Hrim ... Kama-Bana-Vidhvamsanaya Pushpam Nirvapamiti Svaha.
(Translation: I offer this flower for the destruction of the arrow of desire, svaha.)

**Question 53.** Which mantra is recited while offering the food-offering (naivedya)?
Answer: Om Hrim ... Kshudha-Roga-Vinashanaya Naivedyam Nirvapamiti Svaha.
(Translation: I offer this food-offering for the destruction of the affliction of hunger, svaha.)

**Question 54.** Which mantra is recited while offering the lamp (deepa)?
Answer: Om Hrim ... Mohandhakara-Vinashanaya Deepam Nirvapamiti Svaha.
(Translation: I offer this lamp for the destruction of the darkness of delusion, svaha.)

**Question 55.** Which mantra is recited while offering incense (dhoopa)?
Answer: Om Hrim ... Ashta-Karma-Dahanaya Dhoopam Nirvapamiti Svaha.
(Translation: I offer this incense for the burning away of the eight karmas, svaha.)

**Question 56.** Which mantra is recited while offering fruit?
Answer: Om Hrim ... Moksha-Phala-Praptaye Phalam Nirvapamiti Svaha.
(Translation: I offer this fruit for the attainment of the fruit of liberation, svaha.)

**Question 57.** Which mantra is recited while offering the argha (the combined final offering)?
Answer: Om Hrim ... Anargha-Pada-Praptaye Arghyam Nirvapamiti Svaha.
(Translation: I offer this argha for the attainment of the priceless state [of liberation], svaha.)

**Question 58.** What is the first limb of puja?
Answer: Abhisheka (the ceremonial bathing of the image).

**Question 59.** What is the second limb of puja?
Answer: Ahvanana (invocation).

**Question 60.** What is the third limb of puja?
Answer: Sthapana (establishing [the deity's presence]).

**Question 61.** What is the fourth limb of puja?
Answer: Sannidhikarana (drawing near).

**Question 62.** What is the fifth limb of puja?
Answer: Worship with the eight substances (ashta-dravya).

**Question 63.** What is the sixth limb of puja?
Answer: Visarjana (taking leave).

**Question 64.** Write out one argha-verse used in puja.
Answer: "Jala phala adhyam shuchisara tankon argha karon, tumako apon bhavatara bhavatari moksha varon. Chaubison Shri Jinachanda anandakanda sahi. Pad jajata harata bhavaphanda pavata mokshamahi." Om Hrim Shri Vrishabhadi Viranterbhyo Anargha-Pada-Praptaya Argha Nirvapamiti Svaha.
(Translation: With water and fruit, rich and pure, I offer you this argha; O ferry across worldly existence, grant me the boon of liberation. To all twenty-four Jina-lords, the very root of bliss, whose feet — when worshipped — destroy the snare of transmigration and bring one to the great land of liberation [I offer this]. Om Hrim, to the Lords from Rishabha to Mahavira, for the attainment of the priceless state, I offer this argha, svaha.)

**Question 65.** Why is circumambulation (parikrama) performed in the temple?
Answer: The temple is a symbol of the samavasarana (the divine preaching-hall), within which the Lord's face appears in all four directions at once. In order to take his darshan from every side, devotees perform circumambulation.

**Question 66.** Which verse is recited while taking gandhodaka (the scented water used in temple ritual)?
Answer: Nirmalam Nirmalikaranam Pavitram Papa-Nashanam, Jina-Gandhodakam Vande Ashta-Karma-Vinashanam.
(Translation: I bow to the scented water of the Jina — that which is pure, which purifies, which is sacred, which destroys sin, and which destroys the eight karmas.)

# 5. Mahapurush (The Great Personages)

**Question 1.** Who was he who was kept bound in chains?
Answer: Muni Mantunga-acharya.

**Question 2.** Who was he on whose head a burning brazier was placed?
Answer: Gajakumar Muniraj.

**Question 3.** Who were they who were made to wear red-hot iron ornaments?
Answer: The five Pandava Munirajs.

**Question 4.** Who was he around whose neck a dead snake was placed?
Answer: Yashodhar Muniraj.

**Question 5.** Who placed the snake around Muni Yashodhar's neck?
Answer: King Shrenik.

**Question 6.** Who was he who removed the affliction (upasarga) besetting seven hundred Munirajs?
Answer: Vishnukumar Muniraj.

**Question 7.** Which Muniraj suffered from the disease of Bhasmaka (a consuming, unquenchable hunger)?
Answer: Samantabhadra Muniraj.

**Question 8.** Who was he to whom a female jackal came to feed for three days [after his death, licking his blood-covered body — an episode of extreme endurance]?
Answer: Sukumal Muniraj.

**Question 9.** Which Muniraj's wrath caused Dwarka to burn?
Answer: Dvaipayana Muniraj.

**Question 10.** Who was he to whom the gods brought food in the forest?
Answer: Chandragupta Muniraj.

**Question 11.** Which Muniraj was devoured by a tigress?
Answer: Sukaushal Muniraj.

**Question 12.** Which Muniraj engaged in debate with the Buddhists?
Answer: Akalanka Dev Muniraj.

**Question 13.** Who were they who attained liberation before Lord Adinath?
Answer: Anantavirya and Bahubali.

**Question 14.** Who was he whose affliction was removed by Rama and Lakshmana?
Answer: Kulabhushan and Deshabhushan Muniraj.

**Question 15.** Who was he who took renunciation just one day after his marriage?
Answer: Jambu Swami.

**Question 16.** Which Muniraj pressed down Mount Kailash with his supernatural bodily strength (bala-riddhi)?
Answer: Bali Muniraj.

**Question 17.** Which Muniraj was infatuated with his one-eyed wife — and later became free of that attachment?
Answer: Pushpadal Muniraj.

**Question 18.** Who were they who turned to renunciation after being looked at with contempt by their sister?
Answer: Kulabhushan and Deshabhushan.

**Question 19.** Which Muniraj had an affliction inflicted on him by the soul that had been Sita?
Answer: Ram Muni.

**Question 20.** Which Muniraj was pierced with arrows by an enemy [while in meditation]?
Answer: Dhanyakumar Muniraj.

**Question 21.** Who was he who, seated atop an elephant during battle, plucked out his hair [and took renunciation on the spot]?
Answer: King Madhu.

**Question 22.** Who was he whose bodily beauty the gods came down to see?
Answer: Sanatkumar Chakravarti.

**Question 23.** Who was he for whom the execution-stake turned into a throne when he was put upon it?
Answer: Sudarshan Seth.

**Question 24.** Who was the first great donor of this era?
Answer: King Shreyans.

**Question 25.** Who was he whose fall shattered a boulder to pieces?
Answer: Hanuman.

**Question 26.** Who was he whose breath alone made a boulder tremble?
Answer: Pradyumna Kumar.

**Question 27.** Who was he who crossed the ocean by swimming, through his own bodily strength?
Answer: King Shripal.

**Question 28.** Who was he who was crushed under an elephant's foot?
Answer: Pandit Todarmal ji.

**Question 29.** Who was he who fed a lion jalebi (a sweet) instead of meat?
Answer: Amarchand ji Diwan.

**Question 30.** Who was he who gave up his own life for the sake of religion, in order to save his brother's life?
Answer: Nishkalank.

**Question 31.** Who was he who, considering it contrary to scripture, did not offer food to a muni possessing the charana-riddhi (the power of flight)?
Answer: Arhaddatta Seth.

**Question 32.** Who was he who, over four months in the forest, built a city through magical knowledge (vidya) and thereby provided food [to the muni]?
Answer: Bhamandal.

**Question 33.** Who was he who recited the Namokar Mantra to a dog?
Answer: Jivandhar Swami.

**Question 34.** Who was he who, for the sake of justice, refused to side with his own brother?
Answer: Vibhishan.

**Question 35.** Who was he who helped a thief who had broken into his house to carry away his wealth?
Answer: Pandit Banarasidas ji.

**Question 36.** Who was he who became infatuated with a courtesan and went to live in her house?
Answer: Charudatta Seth.

**Question 37.** Who was he who had the image of Lord Bahubali built at Shravanabelgola?
Answer: Chamundaraya.

**Question 38.** Who was he who was born in a prison?
Answer: Shri Krishna.
