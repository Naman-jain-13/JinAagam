# 6. Prabhu Patit Pavan (The Lord, Redeemer of the Fallen)

**Question 1.** Who composed this supplication (vinati) "Prabhu Patitpavan"?
Answer: The supplication "Prabhu Patitpavan" was composed by Pandit Budhjan ji.

**Question 2.** What is the Lord like?
Answer: The Lord is patita-pavana (the purifier of the fallen) — holy.

**Question 3.** What am I like?
Answer: I am fallen (patit) and impure (apavan).

**Question 4.** Give the meaning of the word "virad."
Answer: The word "virad" means greatness, or a eulogy (prashasti).

**Question 5.** Give the meaning of the word "mento."
Answer: The word "mento" means destruction.

**Question 6.** Give the meaning of the word "pichhanya."
Answer: The word "pichhanya" means to recognize.

**Question 7.** What are the various kinds of gods (deva) like?
Answer: The various kinds of gods are sarāgī — still possessed of passion and attachment (unlike the passionless Jina).

**Question 8.** In which forest has whose wealth been plundered by whom?
Answer: In the terrible forest of worldly existence (bhava-vikatavana), my enemy in the form of karma has plundered the wealth that is knowledge.

**Question 9.** Forgetting what, becoming what, have I wandered where?
Answer: Forgetting what is truly desirable (ishta) and becoming corrupted (bhrashta), I have wandered into what is undesirable — evil states of rebirth (durgati).

**Question 10.** What all of mine has become blessed?
Answer: My hour has become blessed, my day has become blessed, and my very birth has become blessed.

**Question 11.** For what reason did I attain the darshan (auspicious sight) of the Lord?
Answer: I attained the Lord's darshan because of the rise of good fortune (bhagyodaya).

**Question 12.** What are the Lord's countenance, posture, and gaze like?

**Question 13.** What is the meaning of the word "vasu"?

**Question 14.** With how many qualities (gunas) is the Lord endowed?

**Question 1.** Who composed "Meri Bhavana"? How many verses does "Meri Bhavana" contain?

**Question 2.** Describe vitaragata (passionlessness) as it appears in "Meri Bhavana."
Answer: Those who have conquered attachment (raga), aversion (dvesha), desire (kama), and the like, are vitaragi (passionless).

**Question 3.** Describe sarvajnata (omniscience) as it appears in "Meri Bhavana."
Answer: They have known the whole universe; those who have known all substances of the loka (universe) and aloka (non-universe) are sarvajna (omniscient).

**Question 4.** Describe hitopadeshita (the giving of beneficial teaching) as it appears in "Meri Bhavana."
Answer: They taught the path of liberation to all beings, desiring nothing for themselves.

**Question 5.** Give the meaning of the word "nihsprih."
Answer: The word "nihsprih" means selflessness.

**Question 6.** Name the synonyms of the Lord.
Answer: Buddha, Vir, Jin, Hari, Har, and Brahma are synonyms of the Lord.

**Question 7.** In what should my mind remain absorbed?
Answer: Roused by devotion, may this mind remain ever absorbed in Him (the Arihant, the Siddha).

**Question 8.** What is called vishaya (sense-object)?
Answer: That which is grasped through the senses is called vishaya.

**Question 9.** What wealth do sadhus (monks) keep?
Answer: Sadhus keep the wealth of equanimity (samyabhav); sadhus keep the wealth that is samata (equanimity of mind).

**Question 10.** In what, and when, are sadhus ever intent?
Answer: They are ever intent, day and night, on accomplishing the good of both self and others.

**Question 11.** What kind of austerity do sadhus practice, and how?
Answer: They practise the difficult austerity of relinquishing self-interest, without any regret.

**Question 12.** What do sadhus do for the world?
Answer: They remove its mass of suffering. Sadhus remove the mass of suffering of the world.

**Question 13.** With whom should there be constant company and constant contemplation?
Answer: May there ever be the company of those sadhus who remove the mass of suffering, and may there ever be contemplation of them alone.

**Question 14.** In what should my mind remain devoted?
Answer: May this mind remain ever devoted to conduct like that of the sadhus.

**Question 22.** State the aspiration for avoiding the passion of anger (krodh-kashaya).

Answer: As far as it is in my power, may I do good to others in this life.

# 9. Paap (Sin / Demerit)

**Question 1.** What is called paap (sin)?
Answer: That which causes the fall of the soul — bringing censure and disrepute in this life, and rebirth in hell in the next — is called paap.

**Question 2.** How many kinds of paap are there?
Answer: There are five: himsa (violence), jhooth (falsehood), chori (theft), kushil (unchastity), and parigraha (possessiveness).

**Question 3.** What is called himsa (violence)?
Answer: To take life while under the sway of carelessness (pramada) is himsa.

**Question 4.** How many kinds of himsa are there?
Answer: Four kinds: sankalpi (intentional), arambhi (arising from household activity), udyogi (arising from occupation), and virodhi (arising from defensive resistance).

**Question 5.** What is called sankalpi himsa?
Answer: Deliberately forming the intention to kill a being. In this, whether the being dies or not, sin is incurred regardless.

**Question 6.** What is called arambhi himsa?
Answer: The violence to the six classes of bodied beings (shatkaya jiva) that occurs through ordinary household activities such as drawing water, lighting fire, and cleaning the house.

**Question 7.** What is called udyogi himsa?
Answer: The violence that occurs in carrying out occupations such as farming, trade, or service/employment.

**Question 8.** What is called virodhi himsa?
Answer: The violence that occurs in resisting an attack in order to protect oneself, one's family, one's religion, or one's nation.

**Question 9.** From which kind of himsa does a shravak (lay householder) abstain?
Answer: From sankalpi himsa.

**Question 10.** If, despite taking every precaution to protect living beings, some still die, is sin incurred?
Answer: No.

**Question 11.** Does sin attach only to the one who kills, or to others as well?
Answer: Through commission (krita), instigation (karita), and approval (anumodana), equal sin attaches to the one who does the deed, the one who causes it to be done, and the one who approves (praises) it.

**Question 12.** What is called jhooth (falsehood)?
Answer: Not to say what one has seen and heard exactly as it was, is falsehood.

**Question 13.** If someone speaks an untruth in order to save another's life, is sin incurred?
Answer: No sin is incurred, because in that case the intention behind the words is to save a life.

**Question 14.** Is it true (i.e., not a fault) to call a blind man blind, or a thief a thief?
Answer: To mock someone, to speak harshly, to be cruel, uncivil, or suspicious in speech, or to slander — these are sins.

**Question 15.** How should we speak?
Answer: We should speak words that are beneficial, measured, and pleasant.

**Question 16.** What is called chori (theft)?
Answer: To take, out of carelessness and without being given, an object that belongs to another, that has fallen, or that has been lost, without informing anyone — this is theft. As it is said: "adattadanam steyam" (taking what is not given is theft) — Tattvartha Sutra 7/15.

**Question 17.** What is called kushil (unchastity)?
Answer: To desire physical contact with a woman or man, out of an attachment-laden (impure) intention, is kushil.

**Question 17 (repeated as printed).** What is called parigraha (possessiveness)?
Answer: To feel infatuation (murchha, a craving of the mind) for a substance, whether sentient or insentient, is parigraha. As it is said: "murchha parigrahah" (attachment is possession) — Tattvartha Sutra 7/17.

**Question 19.** What is called a vrata (vow)? How many kinds are there?
Answer: To renounce the five sins partially is an anuvrata (minor vow), and to renounce them completely is a mahavrata (great vow). There are five vows.

**Question 20.** State the five transgressions (atichara) of the vow of ahimsa.
Answer: Binding (a creature), beating it, loading it with more weight than it can bear, withholding food and water from it, and mutilating it (piercing its nose, ears, and the like).

**Question 21.** State the five transgressions of the satya-anuvrata (vow of truthfulness).
Answer: Mithyopadesh (false instruction), rahovyakhyan (disclosing others' secrets), kutalekhakriya (forgery), nyasapahar (misappropriating a trust/deposit), and sakaramantrabhed (betraying another's confidential intent through gesture).

**Question 22.** What is called mithyopadesh?
Answer: To speak against someone whose reputation is rising, contrary to the truth.

**Question 24.** What is called rahovyakhyan?
Answer: To reveal the private conduct engaged in by a woman or man in seclusion.

**Question 25.** What is called kutalekhakriya?
Answer: To prepare a false document in order to defraud someone.

**Question 26.** What is called nyasapahar?
Answer: When someone entrusted with money, gold, or the like forgets the amount, and the depositor, mistakenly asking for less than was actually left, is told "take it, this is all you left" — that is, returning a deposit in a reduced amount, or not returning it at all.

**Question 27.** What is called sakaramantrabhed?
Answer: To reveal, through some pretext or other means, another's secret intention.

**Question 26 (repeated as printed).** State the five transgressions of the achaurya-vrata (vow of non-stealing).
Answer: Stenaprayog (encouraging theft), tadahritadan (receiving stolen goods), viruddharajyatikram (violating the state's laws), hinadhikamanonman (using false weights and measures), and pratirupakavyavahar (passing off counterfeit goods as genuine).

**Question 27 (repeated as printed).** What is called stenaprayog?
Answer: Suggesting ways to commit theft, praising a thief, and approving of theft.

**Question 28.** What is called tadahritadan?
Answer: Purchasing stolen goods.

**Question 29.** What is viruddharajyatikram?
Answer: Acting against the laws of the state — such as buying a cheap item and selling it at an inflated price, or evading taxes.

**Question 30.** What is called hinadhikamanonman?
Answer: Keeping weights uneven when receiving and giving, using a scale that gives a false weight, and the like.

**Question 31.** What is called pratirupakavyavahar?
Answer: Mixing a counterfeit item in with a genuine one in order to defraud others.

**Question 32.** State the five transgressions of the brahmacharya-anuvrata (minor vow of chastity).
Answer: Parvivahakaran (arranging the marriage of others), aparigrihita-itvarika-gamana (consorting with an unattached/unmarried woman), parigrihita-itvarika-gamana (consorting with another's spouse), anangakrida (unnatural sexual indulgence), and kamativrabhinivesh (intense, excessive attachment to sensual desire).

**Question 33.** What is called parvivahakaran?
Answer: Arranging engagements or marriages, without personal need, for people outside one's own household and family.

**Question 34.** What is called aparigrihita-itvarika-gamana?
Answer: Visiting prostitutes and keeping relations with them.

**Question 35.** What is called parigrihita-itvarika-gamana?
Answer: Visiting and keeping contact with an unfaithful woman who, though married, has illicit relations with other men.

**Question 36.** Why should one eat only during the day?
Answer: Ultraviolet and infrared rays present in sunlight prevent the generation of minute life-forms and thus prevent the arising of disease; in this way, ahimsa (non-violence) is observed.

**Question 37.** What does modern science say on this subject?
Answer: Digestion of food occurs properly only in daylight; therefore one should eat three to four hours before sunset (bedtime).

**Question 38.** What do other religious traditions say about eating at night?
Answer: The Shantiparva of the Mahabharata states that, among the four gateways to hell, night-eating is named as the first gateway. The Markandeya Purana states that drinking water after sunset is equivalent to drinking blood, and eating grain at night is equivalent to eating flesh. The Rishishwar Bharat states that all the vows of one who consumes liquor, meat, food at night, or root vegetables (kanda-mula) come to nothing.

**Question 39.** What harm comes from eating at night?
Answer: Many kinds of illness result. If a fly is present, vomiting-inducing illness results; if even a trace of a spider [enters food], leprosy results; if a louse [enters food], dropsy results; if a hair [enters food], one's voice becomes impaired. In this way, sin of violence is incurred.

**Question 40.** Can one eat at night by the light of a lamp?
Answer: No. Food is not digested properly [when eaten at night], causing gastric trouble, sugar (diabetes), cancer, heart attack, and the like. Nowadays the greater incidence of illness is largely due to eating at night.

**Question 41.** What benefit comes from renouncing night-eating?
Answer: With fewer illnesses, the body stays healthy, so one's wealth is not spent on doctors; peace prevails in the home, and one is spared the sin of violence.

**Question 42.** What is the benefit of giving up all four kinds of food at night?
Answer: It brings the merit of six months of fasting within a year, and leads to the attainment of a good rebirth.

**Question 43.** Why should one not eat root vegetables (jamikand)?
Answer: In a root vegetable such as a potato, as small as the point of a needle, there exist infinite subtle life-forms. To avoid the sin of killing those beings, one should not eat them.

**Question 44.** What should our kitchen (bhojanshala) be like?
Answer: It should be like an operation theatre, where no one is allowed to enter wearing unwashed clothes.

**Question 45.** How should one eat one's food?
Answer: One should not watch television or the like while eating, and one should eat in silence.

**Question 46.** What harm comes from eating while watching television?
Answer: At that time, one's attention is fixed not on the food but on the television, so that one does not even notice what one is eating.

# 10. Janma (Birth)

**Question 1.** What is called janma (birth)?
Answer: The obtaining of a new body upon leaving the previous body is called janma.

**Question 2.** How many kinds of janma are there?
Answer: Three kinds: sammurchhan (spontaneous generation), garbhaja (birth from a womb), and upapada (instantaneous birth).

**Question 3.** What is called sammurchhan janma?
Answer: The formation of a body — without needing the union of the father's and mother's reproductive fluids (raja-virya) — from material particles (pudgala paramanu) suited for it, is sammurchhan janma.

**Question 4.** What is called garbha janma (birth from a womb)?
Answer: The formation of a body in the mother's womb through the union of the father's and mother's reproductive fluids.

**Question 5.** What is called upapada janma?
Answer: The formation of a body, without the union of the parents' reproductive fluids, from material particles at a fixed location (such as an "upapada-shayya," a birth-couch).

**Question 6.** To whom does sammurchhan janma belong?
Answer: All beings from one-sensed up through four-sensed beings are invariably sammurchhan-born. Some five-sensed animals (tiryancha) and undeveloped (aparyapta) human beings can also have sammurchhan birth.

**Question 7.** How many kinds of garbha janma (womb-birth) are there?
Answer: Three kinds: jarayuja, andaja, and potaja.

**Question 8.** What is called jarayuja?
Answer: A being born wrapped in a sac-like membrane resembling flesh and blood, similar to filth (mala).

**Question 9.** What is called andaja?
Answer: A being that is born from an egg.

**Question 10.** What is called potaja?
Answer: Beings that, at the moment of birth, are already free from any such enveloping membrane, and are able to move about immediately.

**Question 11.** To whom does upapada janma belong?
Answer: To celestial beings (deva) and to hell-beings (naraki).

# 11. Avagahana (Bodily Height / Space-Points Occupied by the Soul)

**Question 1.** What is called avagahana?
Answer: The height of the body (utsedha) is called avagahana.

**Question 2.** What is the maximum avagahana of a one-sensed being?
Answer: A little over one thousand yojanas — as in the case of a lotus and the like.

**Question 3.** What is the maximum avagahana of a two-sensed being?
Answer: Twelve yojanas — as in the case of a conch and the like.

**Question 4.** What is the maximum avagahana of a three-sensed being?
Answer: Three kos — as in the case of an ant and the like.

**Question 5.** What is the maximum avagahana of a four-sensed being?
Answer: One yojana — as in the case of a bee and the like.

**Question 6.** What is the maximum avagahana of a five-sensed being?
Answer: One thousand yojanas — as in the case of the mahamatsya (the great fish) and the like.

**Question 7.** What is the maximum avagahana of a human being, and where is it found?
Answer: Three kos, in the finest bhogabhumi (land of enjoyment).

**Question 8.** What is the maximum avagahana of a human being in the karmabhumi (land of action)?
Answer: 525 dhanusha (bow-lengths), as in the case of Bahubali.

**Question 9.** What is the measure of a yojana and of a dhanusha (bow-length)?
Answer: A yojana equals eight miles, and a dhanusha (bow-length) equals four hastas (hand-lengths/cubits).

**Question 10.** What are the minimum and maximum avagahana of hell-beings?
Answer: In the first hell, the minimum avagahana is three hastas, and in the seventh hell, the maximum avagahana is five hundred dhanusha.

**Question 11.** What are the minimum and maximum avagahana of celestial beings?
Answer: The minimum avagahana of the gods of Sarvarthasiddhi is one hasta, and the maximum avagahana of the Asurakumar gods is twenty-five dhanusha.

# 12. Yoni (Birth-Source)

**Question 1.** What is called yoni?
Answer: The basis/site of birth is called yoni.

**Question 2.** How many kinds of yoni are there?
Answer: There are two principal kinds: akara-yoni and guna-yoni.

**Question 3.** How many kinds of akara-yoni are there? Name and describe them.
Answer: Three kinds: shankhavarta, kurmonnata, and vamshapatra. (1) In the shankhavarta yoni, no embryo takes hold. (2) In the kurmonnata yoni, great beings such as the Tirthankaras take birth. (3) In the vamshapatra yoni, ordinary men and women take birth.

**Question 4.** Name and describe the kinds of guna-yoni.
Answer: There are nine kinds: sachitta (associated with living matter), shita (cold), samvrita (concealed), achitta (associated with non-living matter), ushna (hot), vivrita (open/exposed), sachittachitta (mixed living/non-living), shitoshna (mixed cold/hot), and samvritavivrita (mixed concealed/exposed).

**Question 5.** What is called samvrita-yoni?
Answer: A yoni that is not visible, being covered/enclosed, is called samvrita-yoni.

**Question 6.** What is called shita-yoni?
Answer: A yoni possessed of a cold touch is called shita-yoni.

**Question 7.** What is called sachitta-yoni?
Answer: A yoni that possesses life (associated with a living substance) is called sachitta-yoni.

**Question 8.** How many divisions does guna-yoni have by extent, and of what sort?
Answer: By extent, there are 8,400,000 (84 lakh) divisions: one-sensed beings — 52 lakh; two-sensed beings — 2 lakh; three-sensed beings — 2 lakh; four-sensed beings — 2 lakh; five-sensed animals (tiryancha) — 4 lakh; gods — 4 lakh; hell-beings — 4 lakh; and humans — 14 lakh.

**Question 9.** How do the 52 lakh yonis of one-sensed beings arise?
Answer: [They consist of:] the earth-bodied, water-bodied, fire-bodied, and air-bodied beings — each of the general (itara-nigoda) and permanent (nitya-nigoda) categories — 7 lakh each (7×6 = 42 lakh), plus the plant-bodied (vanaspatikaya) beings — 10 lakh. In this way, 42 lakh plus 10 lakh together make 52 lakh divisions.

# 13. Jiva Samasa (Classification of Souls)

**Question 1.** What is called jiva samasa?
Answer: That by which many souls are grouped together (into classes) is called jiva samasa.

**Question 2.** How many divisions does jiva samasa have?
Answer: Jiva samasa has fourteen divisions.

**Question 3.** Are all souls both sukshma (subtle/microscopic) and badar (gross)?
Answer: No — only one-sensed beings have both the sukshma and badar divisions. Beings with mobility (trasa) are invariably badar (gross).

**Question 4.** What are called a badar jiva (gross soul) and a badar body?
Answer: Through the rise of badar-nama-karma, a soul is called badar. A body that obstructs another, or is itself obstructed by another, is called a badar (gross) body.

**Question 5.** What are called a sukshma jiva (subtle soul) and a sukshma body?
Answer: Through the rise of sukshma-nama-karma, a soul is called sukshma. A body that neither obstructs another nor is itself obstructed by another is called a sukshma (subtle) body.

**Question 6.** State the sub-divisions (uttarabheda) of jiva samasa.
Answer: The sub-divisions of jiva samasa number ninety-eight (98).

**Question 7.** Among whom are these ninety-eight sub-divisions of jiva samasa distributed?
Answer: Among the tiryancha (animals) — 85; among humans — 9; among hell-beings (naraki) — 2; and among celestial beings (deva) — 2 — making 98 sub-types in total.
