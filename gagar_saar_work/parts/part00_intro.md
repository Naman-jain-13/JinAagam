# Learn Through Play

*Om Namah Shri Acharya Vardhamansagaraya*

A few years ago, scriptural study (*shastra swadhyaya*) used to take place regularly in Jina temples in the evenings. But nowadays, in this age of material pursuits, dazzled by worldly means, no one has time for such study. Whatever little study does happen is usually confined only to *Adhyatma* (spirituality), *Dravyanuyoga* (the doctrine of substances), and the *Nishchaya-naya* (the absolute standpoint) — leaving people unacquainted with the other subjects of the Agam.

We present in your hands this small book, "**Gagar Mein Sagar**" ("An Ocean in a Pot"). It brings together a very good collection of short question-and-answer sets covering almost all the *Anuyogas* (branches of scriptural exposition). This book will help everyone gain knowledge — from small children to the elderly.

Through this book, religious-minded people of every group can acquire knowledge of the Agam while playing games.

Any brothers and sisters who wish to take part and play these games should be given this book a few days in advance to memorize, so that they can properly remember the answers to its questions. Before that, let them know in advance which topics the questions will be drawn from. Give children topics suited to children, and give the more learned participants topics suited to them. Then begin the game on a fixed date.

**1. Cricket:** Form two teams of 11 players each. One player "bowls" six balls to the opposing "batsman" — that is, asks six questions. If the batsman answers within 5 seconds, it counts as six runs. If he answers between 5 and 10 seconds, it counts as four runs. In this way you may fix the run-values (1 run, 2 runs, etc.) as convenient. If the opposing player cannot answer within the fixed time, he is given out. In this way you can have fun while playing this game.

**2. Antakshari:** Form teams of 5 each, or as convenient, and form groups. An *Antakshari*-style contest is then held among them, as is done in World Cup-style formats for other games, and whoever wins the final round is declared the winner.

**3. Written Examination:** Here you may have candidates fill out a form for whichever examination they wish to take, give them the book, and then hold a written examination at a fixed time. The examiner may prepare the question paper as they see fit, leaving space after each question for the answer to be written in. You may allot 95 marks for the paper and 5 marks for neatness and cleanliness, etc., so as to make it easier to declare first, second place, and so on.

Other similar games may also be played in the same way.
