**Question 38.** With respect to whom is [this duration] anādi-anant (beginningless-and-endless)?
Answer: With respect to the abhavya (a soul incapable of ever attaining liberation).

**Question 39.** With respect to whom is it anādi-sānt (beginningless-but-with-an-end)?
Answer: With respect to the bhavya jīva (a soul capable of liberation, prior to attaining right faith).

**Question 40.** With respect to whom is it sādi-sānt (with-a-beginning-and-with-an-end)?
Answer: With respect to one who, having attained samyagdarśana (right faith), again falls back into mithyādṛṣṭi (wrong faith, the first gunasthana).

**Question 41.** What are the minimum (jaghanya) and maximum (utkṛṣṭa) durations of the second gunasthana?
Answer: The minimum duration is one samaya (instant); the maximum duration is six āvalīs.

**Question 42.** What are the minimum and maximum durations of the third gunasthana?
Answer: Antarmuhūrta (both).

**Question 43.** What are the minimum and maximum durations of the fourth gunasthana?
Answer: The minimum duration is antarmuhūrta, and the maximum duration is a little less than one koṭipūrva plus thirty-three sāgaras (sāgaropama).

**Question 44.** What are the maximum and minimum durations of the fifth gunasthana?
Answer: The minimum duration is antarmuhūrta, and the maximum duration is one koṭipūrva minus an antarmuhūrta. A sammūrchhana (spontaneously-generated) tiryañca (animal) can attain saṃyamāsaṃyama (partial self-restraint, the fifth gunasthana) together with vedaka-samyaktva an antarmuhūrta after birth. With respect to human beings, it is one koṭipūrva minus eight years.

**Question 45.** What are the minimum and maximum durations of the sixth and seventh gunasthanas?
Answer: For both gunasthanas, with respect to death, the minimum duration is one samaya, and the maximum duration is antarmuhūrta.

**Question 46.** What are the minimum and maximum durations of the kṣapaka-śreṇī (the ladder of annihilation of karmas)?
Answer: Antarmuhūrta (both).

**Question 46 [as printed].** What are the minimum and maximum durations for all four stages of the upaśamaka (suppressing) souls?
Answer: With respect to death, the minimum duration is one samaya, and the maximum duration is antarmuhūrta.

**Question 47.** What are the minimum and maximum durations of the thirteenth gunasthana?
Answer: The minimum duration is antarmuhūrta, and the maximum duration is eight years plus a koṭipūrva year minus eight antarmuhūrtas.

**Question 48.** What are the minimum and maximum durations of the ayogakevalī gunasthana (fourteenth)?
Answer: Both the minimum and the maximum duration are antarmuhūrta.

**Question 49.** How many souls are there in the first gunasthana?
Answer: Anantānanta (infinite-times-infinite) souls.

**Question 50.** With respect to human beings, how many souls are in the second gunasthana?
Answer: 52 crore, with respect to human beings.

**Question 51.** With respect to human beings, how many souls are in the third gunasthana?
Answer: 104 crore souls.

**Question 52.** With respect to human beings, how many souls are in the fourth gunasthana?
Answer: 700 crore.

**Question 53.** With respect to human beings, how many souls are in the fifth gunasthana?
Answer: 13 crore souls.

**Question 54.** How many souls are there in the sixth gunasthana?
Answer: 59,398,206 — five crore ninety-three lakh ninety-eight thousand two hundred six.

**Question 55.** How many souls are there in the seventh gunasthana?
Answer: 29,699,103 — two crore ninety-six lakh ninety-nine thousand one hundred three souls.

**Question 56.** How many souls are there in the thirteenth gunasthana?
Answer: 898,502 — eight lakh ninety-eight thousand five hundred two souls.

**Question 57.** How many souls are there in the fourteenth gunasthana?
Answer: 598 souls.

**Question 58.** In the second, third, fourth, and fifth gunasthanas, with respect to each of the four gatis (realms of existence) separately, how many souls are there?
Answer: In each of the four gunasthanas, separately, they number an innumerable part (asaṅkhyātavāṅ bhāga) of a palya (a unit of numerical measure).

**Question 59.** Among those ascending the upaśama-śreṇī (ladder of suppression), how many souls are there in each separate gunasthana?
Answer: In each of the four gunasthanas separately, there are 299.

**Question 60.** For those on the kṣapaka-śreṇī, what is the separate number of souls in each of the four gunasthanas?

**Question 61.** In all four gunasthanas of the upaśama-śreṇī together, how many souls are there in total?
Answer: In all four stages of the upaśama-śreṇī together, there are 1196 souls.

**Question 62.** In the four gunasthanas of the kṣapaka-śreṇī, how many souls are there?
Answer: In all four stages of the kṣapaka-śreṇī together, there are 2392 souls.

Answer: They number an innumerable part of the jagat-śreṇī (a cosmological linear measure).

Answer: There are nine crore minus three (i.e. 89,999,997) munirājas. All of these are bhāvaliṅgī (true ascetics by inner disposition, not merely outward form).

# 20. Karma

**Question 1.** What is called karma?
Answer: Karma is the bonding of karmic pudgala-varganas (material karmic particle-groups) with the soul, occasioned by the soul's own psychic states such as rāga (attachment) and the like.

**Question 2.** How many kinds of karma are there?
Answer: Karma has divisions of 2, 3, 4, 8, 148, and, ultimately, a number equal to innumerable lokas.

**Question 3.** What are the three kinds of karma?
Answer: Dravya-karma (material karma), bhāva-karma (psychic karma), and no-karma (quasi-karma).

**Question 4.** What is called dravya-karma?
Answer: The mass of karmic pudgala [matter] such as jñānāvaraṇa and the rest.

**Question 5.** What is called bhāva-karma?
Answer: Psychic states such as rāga (attachment) and dveṣa (aversion).

**Question 6.** What is called no-karma?
Answer: External instrumental causes such as the body.

**Question 7.** How many divisions does dravya-karma have?
Answer: Eight: jñānāvaraṇa (knowledge-obscuring), darśanāvaraṇa (perception-obscuring), vedanīya (feeling-producing), mohanīya (deluding), āyu (life-span-determining), nāma (body-determining), gotra (status-determining), and antarāya (obstructing).

**Question 8.** What are the two divisions of dravya-karma?
Answer: Ghātiyā (destructive) and aghātiyā (non-destructive).

**Question 9.** What is called ghātiyā karma?
Answer: That which harms the soul's essential (anujīvī) qualities such as jñāna (knowledge), darśana (perception), vīrya (energy), samyaktva (right faith), and so on, is called ghātiyā karma.

**Question 10.** Which karmas are ghātiyā?
Answer: Jñānāvaraṇa, darśanāvaraṇa, mohanīya, and antarāya.

**Question 11.** What is called aghātiyā karma, and which are they?
Answer: That which harms the soul's incidental (pratijīvī) qualities is called aghātiyā karma: āyu, nāma, gotra, and vedanīya.

**Question 12.** How many kinds of bandha (bonding) are there, and what are they?
Answer: Four kinds: prakṛti-bandha (nature-bonding), pradeśa-bandha (quantity-bonding), sthiti-bandha (duration-bonding), and anubhāga-bandha (intensity-bonding).

**Question 13.** What is called prakṛti-bandha?
Answer: The specific relationship of adhesion (saṃśleṣa) of material karmas with the soul is called prakṛti-bandha.

**Question 14.** What is called pradeśa-bandha?
Answer: The measure of how much quantity of karmic pudgala-varganas has come into bondage — that measure is called pradeśa-bandha.

**Question 15.** What is called sthiti-bandha?
Answer: The length of time for which karma remains bound with the soul.

**Question 16.** What is called anubhāga-bandha?
Answer: The potency of karmas in yielding their fruit is called anubhāga-bandha.

**Question 17.** How many divisions does ghātiyā karma have?
Answer: Two: sarvaghāti (totally obscuring) and deśaghāti (partially obscuring).

**Question 18.** Which are the sarvaghāti (totally obscuring) natures (prakṛtis)? Name them.
Answer: Kevala-jñānāvaraṇa, kevala-darśanāvaraṇa, the five [kinds of] nidrā (sleep), the four anantānubandhī [passions], the four apratyākhyāna [passions], the four pratyākhyāna [passions], and mithyātva, samyagmithyātva — 21 natures are sarvaghāti.

**Question 19.** Which are the deśaghāti (partially obscuring) natures? Name them.
Answer: The [remaining] four of jñānāvaraṇa, three of darśanāvaraṇa, the four saṃjvalana (smouldering) passions, the nine no-kaṣāyas (quasi-passions) beginning with laughter, five of antarāya, and the samyaktva-prakṛti — thus 26 natures are deśaghāti.

**Question 20.** What are the divisions of jñānāvaraṇa karma?
Answer: Mati-jñānāvaraṇa, śruta-jñānāvaraṇa, avadhi-jñānāvaraṇa, manaḥparyaya-jñānāvaraṇa, and kevala-jñānāvaraṇa (obscuring sensory, scriptural, clairvoyant, telepathic, and omniscient knowledge respectively).

**Question 21.** What are the divisions of darśanāvaraṇa karma?
Answer: Cakṣu-darśanāvaraṇa, acakṣu-darśanāvaraṇa, avadhi-darśanāvaraṇa, kevala-darśanāvaraṇa (obscuring ocular, non-ocular, clairvoyant, and omniscient perception); and nidrā, nidrā-nidrā, pracalā, pracalā-pracalā, styānagṛddhi (the five degrees of sleep/torpor).

**Question 22.** What are the divisions of vedanīya karma?
Answer: Sātā-vedanīya (pleasure-producing) and asātā-vedanīya (pain-producing).

**Question 23.** How many divisions does mohanīya karma have?
Answer: Darśana-mohanīya (faith-deluding) and cāritra-mohanīya (conduct-deluding).

**Question 24.** How many divisions does darśana-mohanīya have? Name them.
Answer: Three: mithyātva, samyagmithyātva, and samyaktva-prakṛti.

**Question 25.** How many divisions does cāritra-mohanīya have? Name them.
Answer: Cāritra-mohanīya has two divisions: kaṣāya-vedanīya (relating to the passions) and akaṣāya-vedanīya (relating to the quasi-passions).

**Question 26.** How many divisions does kaṣāya-vedanīya have? Name them.
Answer: Sixteen: four anantānubandhī, four apratyākhyāna, four pratyākhyāna, four saṃjvalana — [each set of four being] krodha (anger), māna (pride), māyā (deceit), lobha (greed).

**Question 27.** How many divisions does akaṣāya-vedanīya have? Name them.
Answer: Nine: hāsya (laughter), rati (liking), arati (disliking), śoka (sorrow), bhaya (fear), jugupsā (disgust), puruṣa-veda (male sex-passion), strī-veda (female sex-passion), napuṃsaka-veda (neuter sex-passion).

**Question 28.** How many divisions does āyu-karma have?
Answer: Four: naraka-āyu (hellish life-span), tiryañca-āyu (animal/plant life-span), manuṣya-āyu (human life-span), deva-āyu (celestial life-span).

**Question 29.** How many divisions does nāma-karma have?
Answer: 93: gati (destiny) 4, jāti (class of being) 5, śarīra (body) 5, aṅgopāṅga (limbs/minor limbs) 3, bandhana (bonding) 5, saṅghāta (consolidation) 5, saṃsthāna (shape) 6, saṃhanana (bone-joint structure) 6, sparśa (touch) 8, rasa (taste) 5, gandha (smell) 2, varṇa (colour) 5 (these twenty together called sparśādika), ānupūrvī (migratory-form) 4, vihāyogati (gait) 2; and sūkṣma (subtle), sādhāraṇa (common-bodied), aparyāpta (undeveloped), durbhaga (unfortunate), duḥsvara (harsh-voiced), anādeya (unimposing), aśubha (inauspicious), asthira (unstable), ayaśaskīrti (ill-famed), sthāvara (immobile) — together with the ten opposites of these; and nirmāṇa (organization), agurulaghu (neither-heavy-nor-light), ucchvāsa (respiration), upaghāta (self-injury), paraghāta (injuring-others capacity), ātapa (heat-radiating body), udyota (cool-light-radiating body), and tīrthaṅkara (the Tīrthaṅkara-making nature).

**Question 30.** How many divisions does antarāya-karma have? Name them.
Answer: Five: dānāntarāya (obstructing charity), lābhāntarāya (obstructing gain), bhogāntarāya (obstructing one-time enjoyment), upabhogāntarāya (obstructing repeated enjoyment), vīryāntarāya (obstructing energy).

**Question 31.** How many sub-divisions (uttara-bheda) do the karmas have?
Answer: 148 divisions.

**Question 32.** From the standpoint of non-differentiation (abheda-vivakṣā), how many bandha-prakṛtis (bonding-natures) are there?
Answer: There are 120 bandha-prakṛtis. Within darśana-mohanīya, bonding occurs only through mithyātva — the other two [samyagmithyātva and samyaktva-prakṛti] are merely fragments of it, so [the three] are counted as one. In nāma-karma, the 5 bandhana and 5 saṅghāta — these 10 natures — are subsumed within the 5 śarīra categories; and among varṇa and the rest [touch, taste, smell, colour], bonding occurs for only 4 [representative types].

**Question 33.** How many kinds of aghātiyā karma are there?
Answer: Two kinds: praśasta (auspicious, puṇya) and apraśasta (inauspicious, pāpa).

**Question 34.** How many praśasta (auspicious) natures are there? Which are they?
Answer: 68: sātā-vedanīya, tiryañca-āyu, manuṣya-āyu, deva-āyu, ucca-gotra (high status), and 63 of nāma-karma.

**Question 35.** Which are the 63 praśasta (puṇya) natures of nāma-karma?
Answer: Varṇādi (colour-group) 20, trasādi (mobile-being group) 12, śarīra 5, bandhana 5, saṅghāta 5, aṅgopāṅga 3, the agurulaghu-ṣaṭka (sixfold agurulaghu group) minus upaghāta (5), and manuṣya-gati, manuṣya-gatyānupūrvī, deva-gati, deva-gatyānupūrvī, pañcendriya-jāti, samacatuḥ-saṃsthāna (symmetrical shape), vajra-vṛṣabha-nārāca-saṃhanana (adamantine skeletal structure), and praśasta-vihāyogati (graceful gait).

**Question 36.** What are the 20 of varṇādi?
Answer: Varṇa (colour) 5, rasa (taste) 5, sparśa (touch) 8, gandha (smell) 2.

**Question 37.** Which is the agurulaghu-ṣaṭka?
Answer: Agurulaghu, upaghāta, paraghāta, ucchvāsa, ātapa, udyota.

**Question 38.** What are the 12 of the trasa group?
Answer: Trasa (mobile), bādara (gross), paryāpta (fully developed), pratyeka-śarīra (individual body), sthira (stable), śubha (auspicious), subhaga (fortunate), susvara (sweet-voiced), ādeya (impressive), yaśaḥkīrti (fame), nirmāṇa, tīrthaṅkara.

**Question 39.** Which are the apraśasta (inauspicious) natures, and how many are there?
Answer: 47 of ghātiyā karma, asātā-vedanīya, nīca-gotra (low status), naraka-āyu, and 50 of nāma-karma — 100 apraśasta (pāpa) natures in all.

**Question 40.** Which are the 50 apraśasta natures of nāma-karma?
Answer: Varṇādi 20, saṃsthāna 5, saṃhanana 5, jāti 4, sthāvara-daśaka (the tenfold immobile group) 10, naraka-gati, naraka-gatyānupūrvī, tiryañca-gati, tiryañca-gatyānupūrvī, upaghāta, and apraśasta-vihāyogati.

**Question 41.** What is the sthāvara-daśaka?
Answer: Sthāvara, sūkṣma, sādhāraṇa, aparyāpta, asthira, aśubha, durbhaga, duḥsvara, anādeya, ayaśaskīrti.

**Question 42.** How many karaṇas (operations/processes) pertaining to karma are there?
Answer: Ten: 1. Bandha (bonding), 2. Udaya (fruition), 3. Sattva (continued existence), 4. Utkarṣaṇa (enhancement), 5. Apakarṣaṇa (diminution), 6. Saṅkramaṇa (transformation into a same-class nature), 7. Udīraṇā (premature fruition), 8. Upaśama (suppression), 9. Nidhatti (fixation), 10. Nikācita (total fixation).

**Question 43.** What is called bandha-karaṇa?
Answer: Karmas entering a relationship of occupying the same space-points (ekakṣetrāvagāha) with the soul is called bandha.

**Question 44.** What is called udaya-karaṇa?
Answer: A karma reaching its own [allotted] duration, that is, the time for yielding its fruit having arrived, is called udaya.

**Question 45.** What is called sattva-karaṇa?
Answer: The continued existence of karmic pudgala-varganas in the form of bound karma is called sattva.

**Question 46.** What is called utkarṣaṇa?
Answer: The increase of the duration and intensity of karmas is utkarṣaṇa.

**Question 47.** What is called apakarṣaṇa-karaṇa?
Answer: The decrease of the duration and intensity of karmas is apakarṣaṇa.

**Question 48.** What is called saṅkramaṇa-karaṇa?
Answer: A karmic nature transforming into a nature of the same class (sajāti) is called saṅkramaṇa.

**Question 49.** What is called udīraṇā-karaṇa?
Answer: Bringing forward into the udaya-āvalī (the fruition-line), by the force of apakarṣaṇa, a karma whose time of fruition has not yet arrived, is udīraṇā.

**Question 50.** What is called upaśama-karaṇa?
Answer: The state in which a karma is not brought into the udaya-āvalī, i.e., does not reach the condition of udīraṇā, is upaśama.

**Question 51.** What is called nidhatti-karaṇa?
Answer: A karma that does not undergo the states of udīraṇā and saṅkramaṇa is nidhatti.

**Question 52.** What is called nikācita-karaṇa?
Answer: A karma that does not undergo the states of udīraṇā, saṅkramaṇa, utkarṣaṇa, and apakarṣaṇa is nikācita.

# 21. Bandha Vyucchitti (Cessation of Bondage)

**Question 1.** What is called bandha-vyucchitti (cessation of bonding)?
Answer: Up through the gunasthana at which the bandha-vyucchitti of certain natures (prakṛtis) is said to occur, the bonding of those natures continues; in any gunasthana beyond that, no bonding of them occurs any longer — that is, saṃvara (stoppage) of those natures takes place.

**Question 2.** In the mithyātva gunasthana, how many natures cease being bound?
Answer: Sixteen: (1) mithyātva, (2) huṇḍaka-saṃsthāna (misshapen configuration), (3) napuṃsaka-veda, (4) asaṃprāptāsṛpāṭikā-saṃhanana (the loosest bone-joint structure), (5) ekendriya, (6) dvīndriya, (7) trīndriya, (8) caturindriya, (9) sthāvara, (10) ātapa, (11) sūkṣma, (12) sādhāraṇa, (13) aparyāpta, (14) naraka-gati, (15) naraka-gatyānupūrvī, (16) naraka-āyu.

**Question 3.** In the sāsādana gunasthana, how many natures cease being bound?
Answer: 25 natures: the four anantānubandhī [passions], 4 saṃhananas, 4 saṃsthānas, 3 nidrās, durbhaga, duḥsvara, anādeya, tiryañca-gatyānupūrvī, tiryañca-āyu, strī-veda, nīca-gotra, udyota, and apraśasta-vihāyogati.

**Question 4.** In the asaṃyata gunasthana, how many natures cease being bound?
Answer: 10 natures: manuṣya-gati, manuṣya-gatyānupūrvī, manuṣya-āyu, audārika-śarīra (gross physical body), audārika-aṅgopāṅga, vajra-vṛṣabha-nārāca-saṃhanana, and the four apratyākhyāna [passions].

**Question 5.** In the deśasaṃyata gunasthana, how many natures cease being bound?
Answer: Four: the four pratyākhyāna [passions].

**Question 6.** In the pramatta gunasthana, how many natures cease being bound?
Answer: Six: asthira, aśubha, ayaśaḥkīrti, asātā-vedanīya, arati, śoka.

**Question 7.** In the apramatta gunasthana, how many natures cease being bound?
Answer: One — deva-āyu.

**Question 8.** In the apūrvakaraṇa gunasthana, how many natures cease being bound?
Answer: 36. In the first part [of this gunasthana]: nidrā and pracalā; in the sixth part: 30 of nāma-karma — tīrthaṅkara, nirmāṇa, praśasta-vihāyogati, pañcendriya-jāti, taijasa-śarīra, kārmaṇa-śarīra, āhāraka-śarīra, vaikriyika-śarīra, āhāraka-aṅgopāṅga, vaikriyika-aṅgopāṅga, sparśa, rasa, gandha, varṇa, agurulaghu, upaghāta, paraghāta, ucchvāsa, trasa, bādara, paryāpta, sthira, pratyeka-śarīra, śubha, susvara, subhaga, ādeya, deva-gati, deva-gatyānupūrvī, samacatuḥ-saṃsthāna; in the seventh part: hāsya, rati, bhaya, jugupsā.

**Question 9.** In the anivṛttikaraṇa gunasthana, how many natures cease being bound?
Answer: 5. In the first part: puruṣa-veda; second part: saṃjvalana krodha; third part: saṃjvalana māna; fourth part: saṃjvalana māyā; fifth part: saṃjvalana lobha.

**Question 10.** In the sūkṣmasāmparāya gunasthana, how many natures cease being bound?
Answer: 16: 5 of jñānāvaraṇa, 4 of darśanāvaraṇa, 5 of antarāya, yaśaḥkīrti, ucca-gotra.

**Question 11.** In the sayogakevalī gunasthana, how many natures cease being bound?
Answer: One — sātā-vedanīya.

# 22. Udaya Vyucchitti (Cessation of Karmic Fruition)

**Question 1.** What is called udaya-vyucchitti (cessation of fruition)?
Answer: The fruition (udaya) of a nature continues only up through the gunasthana at which its udaya-vyucchitti is stated to occur; beyond that, it does not occur in any gunasthana.

**Question 2.** In the mithyātva gunasthana, how many natures cease fruition?
Answer: According to Ācārya Puṣpadanta and Bhūtabali, 10: mithyātva, ātapa, sūkṣma, sādhāraṇa, aparyāpta, sthāvara, ekendriya, and the vikalatraya (the two-, three-, and four-sensed classes). According to Ācārya Yativṛṣabhācārya, 5: mithyātva, ātapa, sūkṣma, sādhāraṇa, aparyāpta.

**Question 3.** In the sāsādana gunasthana, how many natures cease fruition?
Answer: According to Ācārya Puṣpadanta and Bhūtabali, the four anantānubandhī. According to Ācārya Yativṛṣabhācārya, 9: the four anantānubandhī, sthāvara, and the four jātis beginning with ekendriya.

**Question 4.** In the miśra gunasthana, how many natures cease fruition?
Answer: One — samyaktva-mithyātva (the mixed faith-nature).

**Question 5.** In the asaṃyata gunasthana, how many natures cease fruition?
Answer: 17: the vaikriyika-aṣṭaka (eightfold vaikriyika group), the four apratyākhyāna passions, tiryañca-gatyānupūrvī, manuṣya-gatyānupūrvī, durbhaga, anādeya, ayaśaḥkīrti.

**Question 6.** What is the vaikriyika-aṣṭaka? Name them.
Answer: Deva-gati, deva-gatyānupūrvī, naraka-gati, naraka-gatyānupūrvī, vaikriyika-śarīra, vaikriyika-aṅgopāṅga, naraka-āyu, deva-āyu.

**Question 7.** In the deśasaṃyata gunasthana, how many natures cease fruition?
Answer: Eight: the four pratyākhyāna, tiryañca-gati, tiryañca-āyu, udyota, nīca-gotra.

**Question 8.** In the pramatta gunasthana, how many natures cease fruition?
Answer: Five: the āhāraka-pair (āhāraka-śarīra and āhāraka-aṅgopāṅga), nidrā-nidrā, pracalā-pracalā, styānagṛddhi.

**Question 9.** In the apramatta gunasthana, how many natures cease fruition?
Answer: Four: samyaktva-prakṛti and the three inferior saṃhananas.

**Question 10.** In the apūrvakaraṇa gunasthana, how many natures cease fruition?
Answer: Six no-kaṣāyas: hāsya, rati, arati, śoka, bhaya, jugupsā.

**Question 11.** In the anivṛttikaraṇa gunasthana, how many natures cease fruition?
Answer: Six: the three vedas, and saṃjvalana krodha, māna, māyā.

**Question 12.** In the sūkṣmasāmparāya gunasthana, how many natures cease fruition?
Answer: One — sūkṣma lobha (subtle greed).

**Question 13.** In the upaśāntamoha gunasthana, how many natures cease fruition?
Answer: Two: nārāca and vajra-nārāca saṃhanana.

**Question 14.** In the kṣīṇamoha gunasthana, how many natures cease fruition?
Answer: 16: nidrā, pracalā, 5 of jñānāvaraṇa, 4 of darśanāvaraṇa, 5 of antarāya.

**Question 15.** In the sayogakevalī gunasthana, how many natures cease fruition?
Answer: 29: (1) vajra-vṛṣabha-nārāca-saṃhanana, (2) nirmāṇa, (3) sthira, (4) asthira, (5) śubha, (6) aśubha, (7) susvara, (8) duḥsvara, (9) praśasta-vihāyogati, (10) apraśasta-vihāyogati, (11) audārika-śarīra, (12) taijasa-śarīra, (13) kārmaṇa-śarīra, (14) audārika-aṅgopāṅga, (15–24) the 6 saṃsthānas and 4 of varṇādi, (25) agurulaghu, (26) upaghāta, (27) paraghāta, (28) ucchvāsa, (29) pratyeka-śarīra.
*Note:* According to Ācārya Yativṛṣabhācārya, the fruition of one of sātā or asātā [vedanīya] also ceases here, so according to him there are 30 natures.

**Question 16.** In the ayogakevalī gunasthana, how many natures cease fruition?
Answer: 13: sātā-asātā-vedanīya, ucca-gotra, manuṣya-āyu, manuṣya-gati, trasa, bādara, pañcendriya-jāti, subhaga, paryāpta, ādeya, yaśaḥkīrti, tīrthaṅkara.
*Note:* Only one of sātā or asātā [ceases here], since the fruition of one of the two has already ceased at the sayogakevalī stage; hence [effectively] 12.

# 23. Naya (Standpoints / Viewpoints)

**Question 1.** What is called naya (a standpoint)?
Answer: The knowledge that grasps one attribute (aspect) of an object already properly ascertained through pramāṇa (a valid means of knowledge) is called naya; or, the intention of the knower is called naya.

**Question 2.** What are the main divisions of naya, and what is the basis of each?
Answer: There are two divisions. The basis of niścaya-naya (the essential/real standpoint) is dravyārthika-naya (the substance-viewpoint); the basis of vyavahāra-naya (the practical/conventional standpoint) is paryāyārthika-naya (the modification-viewpoint).

**Question 3.** How many specific divisions of naya are there?
Answer: Nine specific divisions: naigama, saṅgraha, vyavahāra, ṛjusūtra, śabda, samabhirūḍha, evambhūta, dravyārthika, and paryāyārthika.

**Question 4.** How many kinds of dravyārthika-naya are there?
Answer: Three: naigama, saṅgraha, and vyavahāra.

**Question 5.** How many divisions does paryāyārthika-naya have?
Answer: Four: ṛjusūtra, śabda, samabhirūḍha, evambhūta.

**Question 6.** What is called naigama-naya?
Answer: The standpoint that grasps a mere intention (saṅkalpa) regarding an object not yet fully accomplished is naigama-naya. For example, if a woman in the kitchen taking out rice and lentils is asked "what are you doing?" she answers "I am cooking rice" — describing the not-yet-completed act by its intended end.

**Question 7.** What is called saṅgraha-naya?
Answer: That which takes as its object an entire collection of things without differentiation is saṅgraha-naya. For example, when one says "jīva" (soul), all souls are meant.

**Question 8.** What is called vyavahāra-naya?
Answer: That which treats, by way of distinction, the object grasped [undifferentiated] by saṅgraha-naya is vyavahāra-naya. For example, having grasped "jīva" through saṅgraha-naya, dividing it as far as distinctions go — into saṃsārī (transmigrating) and mukta (liberated), and further dividing saṃsārī into manuṣya, deva, nāraki, tiryañca, and so on — this dividing is called vyavahāra-naya.

**Question 9.** What is called ṛjusūtra-naya?
Answer: The naya that grasps only objects belonging to the present time, without grasping past or future objects, is called ṛjusūtra-naya. The present is merely a single instant (samaya), and ṛjusūtra-naya takes as its object only the modification (paryāya) of that instant. For example, if one asks a person who has [just] stopped, "Where are you coming from?", the proper answer is "I am not coming from anywhere," because at the very moment the question is asked, the act of coming is not actually occurring.

**Question 10.** What is called śabda-naya?
Answer: The naya that takes as its primary object a word established grammatically through a stem (prakṛti) and suffix (pratyaya) is śabda-naya. This naya removes confusion arising from differences of gender, number, tense, case, person, and voice/aspect.

**Question 11.** What is called samabhirūḍha-naya?
Answer: The naya that takes up mutually-applied (abhirūḍha) synonymous words is samabhirūḍha-naya. In the domain of this naya, even where there is a difference of words, there is no difference of meaning. For example, Śakra, Indra, and Purandara — these three words, being synonyms for devarāja (king of the gods), are all applied to devarāja alone.

**Question 12.** What is called evambhūta-naya?
Answer: The naya in which the actually-occurring present action is given primary importance is evambhūta-naya. The view that it is proper to apply a word only at the moment the object is actually engaged in the activity that word denotes, and not at any other time, is evambhūta-naya. For example, calling someone a "pūjārī" (worshipper) only while he is actually performing worship.

**Question 13.** How many divisions does paryāya (modification) have? Which are they?
Answer: Two: artha-paryāya (subtle/momentary modification) and vyañjana-paryāya (durable/manifest modification).

**Question 14.** What is called artha-paryāya?
Answer: The subtle modification that perishes every instant and is beyond the reach of speech is called artha-paryāya.

**Question 15.** What is called vyañjana-paryāya?
Answer: The gross modification that endures for a long time, is expressible in speech, and is perceptible even to a person of limited knowledge, is called vyañjana-paryāya.

**Question 16.** How many kinds of artha-paryāya are there? Name them.
Answer: Two: (1) svabhāva-artha-paryāya (intrinsic momentary modification) and (2) vibhāva-artha-paryāya (extrinsically-conditioned momentary modification).

**Question 17.** In which substances does vibhāva-artha-paryāya occur?
Answer: Svabhāva-artha-paryāya occurs in all substances (dravyas), but vibhāva-artha-paryāya occurs only in the two substances jīva (soul) and pudgala (matter).

**Question 18.** What is the soul's svabhāva-paryāya?
Answer: In the soul there is the svabhāva-paryāya in the form of jīvatva (the intrinsic nature of sentience), and there is also the vibhāva-paryāya produced by karma.

**Question 19.** How many divisions does vyañjana-paryāya have? Name them.
Answer: Four: vibhāva-dravya-vyañjana-paryāya, vibhāva-guṇa-vyañjana-paryāya, svabhāva-dravya-vyañjana-paryāya, and svabhāva-guṇa-vyañjana-paryāya.

**Question 20.** Explain vibhāva-dravya-vyañjana-paryāya with an example.
Answer: The vyañjana-paryāya of a substance transformed into an extrinsically-conditioned (vibhāva) form is vibhāva-vyañjana-paryāya. For example, states such as being a human or a celestial being are produced by the rise of gati- and āyu-karma, and arise through an overpowering of the soul's own natural state — hence they are vibhāva-paryāya.

**Question 21.** Give an example of vibhāva-guṇa-vyañjana-paryāya.
Answer: Kumati (wrong sensory knowledge), kuśruta (wrong scriptural knowledge), kuavadhi (wrong clairvoyant knowledge), mati, śruta, avadhi, and manaḥparyaya — these seven kinds of knowledge — and cakṣu-, acakṣu-, and avadhi-darśana — these three kinds of perception — are the soul's vibhāva-guṇa-vyañjana-paryāya.

**Question 22.** Explain svabhāva-dravya-vyañjana-paryāya and svabhāva-guṇa-vyañjana-paryāya with examples.
Answer: The siddha-state (a form slightly smaller than the soul's last physical body) is the soul's svabhāva-dravya-vyañjana-paryāya; and the ananta-catuṣṭaya (the fourfold infinitude — infinite knowledge, perception, bliss, and power) is the soul's svabhāva-guṇa-vyañjana-paryāya.

**Question 23.** What are paryāyārthika-naya and dravyārthika-naya?
Answer: From the standpoint of paryāyārthika-naya, substances necessarily arise and perish at every moment; from the standpoint of dravyārthika-naya, they are always of an unperishing and non-arising (eternal) nature.

**Question 24.** What are the two root divisions of naya?
Answer: Niścaya-naya (or dravyārthika-naya) and vyavahāra-naya (or paryāyārthika-naya). The terms vyavahāra, vikalpa, bheda, and paryāya are all synonyms.

**Question 25.** What is called vyavahāra-naya, and how many divisions does it have? Name them.
Answer: The standpoint by which a thing is treated through distinction (bheda) and figurative attribution (upacāra) is vyavahāra-naya. It has three divisions: sadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya (dealing with a thing's real attributes), asadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya (attributing to a thing what does not truly belong to it), and upacarita-asadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya (figuratively attributing what neither belongs to nor truly resembles the thing).

**Question 26.** What is called niścaya-naya?
Answer: The naya that ascertains a thing through non-distinction (abheda) and literal, non-figurative treatment (anupacāratā) is niścaya-naya.

**Question 27.** What is called sadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya?
Answer: The naya that distinguishes a quality (guṇa) from its possessor (guṇī) on the basis of differences of name, number, defining characteristic, and purpose is sadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya. It has two divisions: śuddha-sadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya (pure) and aśuddha-sadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya (impure).

**Question 28.** What are śuddha and aśuddha sadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya?
Answer: Stating the distinction between the pure soul (independent of karmic conditioning) as guṇī and its kṣāyika (karma-destruction-born) pure knowledge-quality, or between the siddha soul and the siddha-state, is śuddha-sadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya; stating the modification/state of an impure (karma-bound) soul is aśuddha-sadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya.

**Question 29.** What is called asadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya?
Answer: Attributing a property properly belonging to one thing onto some other thing is asadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya. It has three divisions: svajātya-sadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya, vijātya-sadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya, and svajāti-vijātya-sadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya. *(As printed, these three sub-names retain the term "sadbhūta"; the source text names them this way even though they fall under the asadbhūta category.)*

**Question 30.** What is called svajātya-sadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya?
Answer: The naya that attributes to a substance a property arising from its relation with other substances of its own kind (svajātīya) is svajātya-sadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya. For example, saying "the paramāṇu (atom) is many-space-pointed" — an atom can become many-space-pointed only through combination with other atoms of its own kind.

**Question 31.** What is called vijātya-sadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya?
Answer: The naya that ascribes a property of one kind to a substance of a different kind is vijātya-sadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya. For example, saying that kṣāyopaśamika mati-jñāna (sensory knowledge arising from the partial destruction-cum-suppression of the corresponding karmas) — itself produced through the kṣayopaśama of the corporeal (mūrtika) mati-jñānāvaraṇa-karma and vīryāntarāya-karma — is itself mūrtika (corporeal): here the quality of corporeality (mūrtitva), which properly belongs to matter (pudgala), is attributed to the soul-quality of knowledge. [The text further notes that] describing the body of a living being as itself the soul is likewise this kind of naya.

**Question 32.** What is called svajāti-vijātya-sadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya?
Answer: Since jīva and ajīva are both objects (viṣaya) of knowledge, figuratively attributing the nature of the knowing subject (viṣayī) to the object — and thereby calling the knowable jīva and ajīva themselves "knowledge" — is svajāti-vijātya-sadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya.

**Question 33.** What is called upacarita-sadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya? How many divisions does it have?
Answer: That which applies a further figurative attribution on top of an [already] figurative attribution is upacarita-sadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya. *(The source text's list of its three sub-divisions is unclear at this point in the scan; it appears to distinguish a truthfully-figurative, an untruthfully-figurative, and a non-distinguishing figurative form.)*

**Question 34.** What is called satya (svajāti) upacarita-asadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya?
Answer: Saying "[my] son, wife, and so on are mine — I am their master."

**Question 35.** What is called vijātyu (asatya) upacarita-sadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya?
Answer: Saying "clothes, ornaments, gold, silver, jewels, and so on are mine — I am their owner."

**Question 36.** What is called svajāti-vijātyupacarita-asadbhūta-vyavahāra-naya?
Answer: Saying "the country, the kingdom, the fort, and so on are mine."
